\begin{abstract}
Fix distinct real roots $2\le r_1<\cdots<r_L$.  We prove a
degree-independent lower bound on the logarithmic coefficient mass of every
real polynomial divisible by $\prod_i(x-r_i)$ whose leading coefficient has
absolute value at least one.  We compare the infinite $\ell^1$ tails of
normalized exponential sums with prescribed positive-integer zeros and
characterize equality for consecutive zeros.  Iterating this comparison
while excluding the largest coefficients bounds successive coefficient
order statistics.  The argument builds on the reduced-height certificate
method of Dubickas and Jankauskas.  For the first $L$ primes the resulting
mass bound is $\Omega(L^2\log L)$.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Polynomial multiples; coefficient mass; reduced
height; Chebyshev systems; exponential sums.

\noindent\textbf{2020 Mathematics Subject Classification.} Primary 11C08.

\section{Introduction}

For $F(x)=\sum_j f_jx^j$, define its logarithmic coefficient mass by
\[
  \Lambda(F)=\sum_{j:f_j\ne0}\log^+|f_j|,
  \qquad \log^+x=\max(0,\log x),
\]
where all logarithms are natural.
Throughout, $\N_{>0}=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and every asymptotic statement is for
$L\to\infty$ with absolute implied constants unless parameters are named.
Unlike the height $\max_j|f_j|$ and the length $\sum_j|f_j|$, $\Lambda(F)$
measures only coefficients whose magnitude exceeds one.  We study how large
$\Lambda(F)$ must be when $F$ is an arbitrary-degree multiple of a polynomial
with prescribed positive roots.

Reduced length and reduced height minimize those two classical coefficient
norms over monic multiples~\cite{dubickas,schinzel,schinzel-complex,dj}.
Prescribed-vanishing problems with low height form a related line of
work~\cite{bombieri-vaaler}.  Dubickas and
Jankauskas associate to reciprocal roots and a prescribed zero set $J$ a
normalized determinant sequence $S_n(J)$.  Their sequence is the
exponential sum used below; its tail gives a dual lower bound for reduced
height, and they compute the consecutive set $J=\{1,\ldots,c-1\}$
explicitly~\cite[Section~2]{dj}.  We prove a prefix-sensitive comparison for
all reciprocal roots at most $1/2$, with equality only for the fully
consecutive zero set.  Iterating the corresponding certificates yields a
distributional bound on many coefficients, not only the largest one.

The zero-counting input is classical Chebyshev-system theory
\cite[Chapter~I, Theorem~4.1]{karlin-studden}.  Related sparse-multiple work
asks for multiples under prescribed degree, sparsity, and height
constraints~\cite{grt}; our bound is uniform in the degree and in all
additional roots of the multiple.

\section{A displaced-zero tail theorem}

Let $\rho_1>\cdots>\rho_c\ge2$ and $y_i=\rho_i^{-1}$.  For any
$Z\subset\N_{>0}$ of size $c-1$, the generalized Vandermonde system uniquely
determines the sum below.  Indeed, a kernel vector would give a nonzero
$c$-node exponential sum with the $c$ zeros $\{0\}\cup Z$, contradicting the
Chebyshev zero bound.
\[
  u_d=\sum_{i=1}^c a_i y_i^d,
  \qquad u_0=1,
  \qquad u_z=0\quad(z\in Z).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Displaced-zero comparison]\label{thm:tail}
Let
$f=\max\{0\le k\le c-1:\{1,\ldots,k\}\subseteq Z\}$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
  \Tail(u):=\sum_{d\ge1}|u_d|
  \le \prod_{i=1}^{f+1}\frac{y_i}{1-y_i}
  =\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{f+1}(\rho_i-1)}.
\end{equation}
Equality holds exactly when $Z=\{1,\ldots,c-1\}$.
\end{theorem}

For a concrete two-node example, take $(\rho_1,\rho_2)=(3,2)$.  If the
prescribed zero is consecutive, $Z=\{1\}$, then
\[
 u_d=3^{1-d}-2^{1-d},\qquad \Tail(u)=\frac12,
\]
so equality holds in \eqref{eq:tail}.  Moving the zero to $Z=\{2\}$ gives
\[
 u_d=\frac95\,3^{-d}-\frac45\,2^{-d},\qquad
 \Tail(u)=\frac3{10}<\frac12.
\]
The theorem generalizes this strict loss.  Each displaced zero creates an
extra sign change relative to a comparison sum, and the proof shows that this
change reduces the total tail.  It treats the displaced zeros one at a time.

A nonzero exponential sum on $r$ distinct positive nodes has at most $r-1$
real zeros, counted with multiplicity.  This follows by dividing by the
smallest node to the real power and applying Rolle's theorem inductively.
Thus an $r$-node sum with $r-1$ specified zeros has no others and changes
sign at each specified zero.

\begin{proof}
Induct on the number $c-1-f$ of displaced zeros.  For
$Z=\{1,\ldots,c-1\}$, put
\[
 U(x)=\sum_{d\ge0}u_dx^d
     =\frac{N(x)}{\prod_i(1-y_ix)},\qquad \deg N\le c-1.
\]
The first $c-1$ nonconstant coefficients vanish.  Comparing the coefficient
of $x^c$ gives, for $d\ge c$,
\[
 u_d=(-1)^{c+1}\Bigl(\prod_i y_i\Bigr)
        h_{d-c}(y_1,\ldots,y_c),
\]
where $h_j$ is the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial.  Hence
\[
 \Tail(u)=\Bigl(\prod_i y_i\Bigr)\sum_{j\ge0}h_j(y_1,\ldots,y_c)
          =\prod_i\frac{y_i}{1-y_i}.
\]

Now suppose a zero is displaced.  Set $z=\max Z$,
$Z'=Z\setminus\{z\}$, and $m=\max Z'$, taking $m=0$ if $Z'$ is empty.
Let $F$ be the sum on $y_1,\ldots,y_{c-1}$ satisfying $F_0=1$ and
$F|_{Z'}=0$.  Let $G$ be the sum on all $c$ nodes satisfying
$G|_{\{0\}\cup Z'}=0$ and $G_z=1$.  Then
\[
  u=F+\lambda G,\qquad \lambda=-F_z.
\]
The induction hypothesis bounds $\Tail(F)$ by the right side of
\eqref{eq:tail}.  It remains to prove $\Tail(u)<\Tail(F)$.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Sign comparison.}
Put $\eta=\operatorname{sgn}(F_z)$ and $\mu=|F_z|$.  On $(0,\infty)$ and
away from $Z'$, the two sequences $F$ and $G$ have constant relative sign
$\eta$: each has exactly the simple zeros $Z'$, so both flip at precisely the
same indices.  At $d=0$, $u_0=F_0=1$ and $G_0=0$; this anchors the comparison
below $z$.
The extra zero of $u$ at $z$ makes $u$ change sign where $F$ does not.
Hence
\[
 |u_d|=\bigl||F_d|-\mu|G_d|\bigr|.
\]
Moreover $|F_d|>\mu|G_d|$ below $z$, with the reverse inequality above
$z$.
For an exponential sum $V$, write
\[
 \Gamma_V(z)=\frac{\sum_{d\ge z}|V_d|}{|V_z|},
 \qquad A_G=\sum_{1\le d<z}|G_d|.
\]
Splitting at $z$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split}
 \Tail(F)-\Tail(u)
 =\mu\bigl(2\Gamma_F(z)-\Gamma_G(z)+A_G\bigr).
\end{equation}

We claim
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma}
 \Gamma_G(z)\le\frac{\Gamma_F(z)}{1-y_c}\le2\Gamma_F(z).
\end{equation}
Let $\widehat F_d=\eta F_d>0$ for $d>m$ and
$E_d=G_d-y_cG_{d-1}$, an exponential sum on the first $c-1$ nodes.  We first
show
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drive}
 \frac{E_d}{\widehat F_d}\le
 \frac{G_z}{\widehat F_z}\qquad(d\ge z>m).
\end{equation}
Write
\[
 A=\{0\}\cup Z'=\{a_1=0<\cdots<a_{c-1}=m\},\qquad
 \sigma=\frac{G_{m+1}}{\widehat F_{m+1}},
\]
and set $\Delta_d=\sigma\widehat F_d-E_d$.  The sum $\Delta$ has at most
$c-2$ real zeros, counted with multiplicity, unless it vanishes identically.
It vanishes at $m+1$ and when
$d-1,d\in A$.  At every remaining marked point $a_{j+1}\in Z'$,
\[
 \Delta_{a_{j+1}}=y_cG_{a_{j+1}-1},\qquad
 \operatorname{sgn}\Delta_{a_{j+1}}=(-1)^{c-1-j}.
\]
Let $r$ be the number of forced zeros from consecutive pairs and let $J$
contain the remaining indices $j$ for which $a_{j+1}\in Z'$.  Then
$|J|=c-2-r$.  If $J$ is empty, the $r$ forced zeros together with $m+1$
exceed the zero budget, so $\Delta$ vanishes identically.  Otherwise take
consecutive $j<j'$ in $J$.  The open interval between their marked points
contains $j'-j-1$ forced zeros, while the endpoint signs differ by
$(-1)^{j'-j}$.  The intervening zero multiplicity must have the parity of $j'-j$
and is therefore at least $j'-j$.  Summing over consecutive members of $J$
therefore contributes one additional zero multiplicity beyond the forced
multiplicities.  Adding these contributions and the zero at $m+1$ gives
\[
 r+(|J|-1)+1=c-2
\]
zero multiplicities, exhausting the zero bound.  Let $a_{j+1}$ be the last
marked point indexed by $J$.  Equality in the count leaves no further zeros
after it and forces every counted zero after it, including $m+1$, to be
simple; earlier forced zeros need not be simple.  There are $c-j-1$ such
sign changes from $a_{j+1}$ through $m+1$.  Since the displayed sign at
$a_{j+1}$ is $(-1)^{c-1-j}$, $\Delta$ is positive immediately after $m+1$.
It has no later zero, so $\Delta_d\ge0$ for $d\ge m+1$.  Therefore
$E_d/\widehat F_d\le\sigma$.  The $c$-node sum
\[
 W_d=\widehat F_{m+1}G_d-G_{m+1}\widehat F_d
\]
vanishes on $Z'\cup\{m+1\}$, exhausting its $c-1$ possible zeros.  It is
positive beyond $m+1$: asymptotically only $G$ contains the largest node
$y_c$, with positive coefficient there.  Hence
$\sigma\le G_z/\widehat F_z$, proving \eqref{eq:drive}.

Induction in $G_d=y_cG_{d-1}+E_d$ now gives
\[
 \frac{G_{z+t}}{G_z}
 \le\sum_{j=0}^t y_c^{t-j}
       \frac{\widehat F_{z+j}}{\widehat F_z}.
\]
Summing over $t$ proves the first inequality in \eqref{eq:gamma}; the second
uses $y_c\le1/2$.  Finally $A_G>0$: if $z>m+1$, then $G_{m+1}\ne0$; if
$z=m+1$, displacement leaves a gap below $z$ where $G$ is nonzero.  Thus
\eqref{eq:split} is strict.  Equality occurs only in the consecutive base
case.
\end{proof}

For example, when $c=2$, $\rho_1=3$, $\rho_2=2$, and $Z=\{1\}$, solving
$u_0=1,u_1=0$ gives $u_d=3(1/3)^d-2(1/2)^d$.  Hence
$\Tail(u)=1/((3-1)(2-1))=1/2$, illustrating both the consecutive-zero
formula and its equality case.

The restriction $\rho_i\ge2$ is substantive.  For $c=2$,
$y_1=1/10$, $y_2=4/5$, and $Z=\{2\}$, the normalized coefficients are
$a_1=64/63$ and $a_2=-1/63$, so
$\Tail(u)=364/2835>1/9=y_1/(1-y_1)$.

\section{Coefficient order statistics}

Let
\[
 P(x)=\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i),\qquad 2\le r_1<\cdots<r_L,
\]
and let $F(x)=\sum_{j=0}^D f_jx^j\in\R[x]$ be a nonzero multiple of $P$.

\begin{lemma}[Slack certificate]\label{lem:slack}
Assume $|f_D|=1$.  Fix $0\le u\le(L-1)/2$ and any set $U$ of $u$
coefficient positions below $D$.  Some $j<D$ outside $U$ satisfies
\[
 |f_j|\ge\prod_{i=2u+1}^L(r_i-1).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $c=L-u$ and use the $c$ largest roots.  A position $j$ has backward
distance $d=D-j$.  Choose a $c$-node sum $w_d$ with $w_0=1$ whose $c-1$
zeros are the distances belonging to $U$ together with the first
$L-2u-1$ positive distances not occupied by $U$.  A weighted sum of the
equations $F(r_i)=0$ gives
\[
 1\le\sum_{j<D,\,j\notin U}|f_jw_{D-j}|.
\]
The chosen zero set starts with at least $L-2u-1$ consecutive distances, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:tail} gives
\[
 \sum_{j<D,\,j\notin U}|w_{D-j}|
 \le\frac{1}{\prod_{i=2u+1}^L(r_i-1)}.
\]
The conclusion follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Logarithmic mass]\label{thm:mass}
If $|f_D|\ge1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass}
 \Lambda(F)\ge
 \sum_{u=0}^{\lfloor(L-1)/2\rfloor}
 \sum_{i=2u+1}^L\log(r_i-1).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Scale to $|f_D|=1$.  For each $u$, let $U$ contain the positions of the $u$
largest coefficients below the leading term.  Lemma~\ref{lem:slack} bounds
the $(u+1)$st largest coefficient by the corresponding product.  Summing
these logarithms proves \eqref{eq:mass}.  Restoring a leading coefficient of
absolute value at least one cannot decrease $\Lambda(F)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Prime roots]
Assume $|f_D|\ge1$.  If $r_i$ is the $i$th prime, or if the increasing
$r_i$ are bounded below by the $i$th primes, then
$\Lambda(F)=\Omega(L^2\log L)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The term $\log(r_i-1)$ occurs once for each $u$ with $2u+1\le i$, hence
exactly $\lceil i/2\rceil$ times in \eqref{eq:mass}.  The elementary bound
$r_i\ge i+1$ therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^L\lceil i/2\rceil\log(r_i-1)=\Omega(L^2\log L).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Confluent analogue]\label{prop:confluent}
Let $0<y_1<\cdots<y_c\le1/2$ and $e_1,\ldots,e_c\ge1$, put $m=e_1+\cdots+e_c$,
and let $y_{(1)}\le\cdots\le y_{(m)}$ be the multiset with each $y_i$ repeated
$e_i$ times.  Let
\[
  u_d=\sum_{i=1}^c P_i(d)y_i^d,\qquad \deg P_i<e_i,
\]
satisfy $u_0=1$ and $u_z=0$ for a set $Z$ of $m-1$ positive integers with
leading run $f$.  Then
\[
  \Tail(u)\le\prod_{j=1}^{f+1}\frac{y_{(j)}}{1-y_{(j)}}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Perturb the repeated nodes into distinct ones: for small $\varepsilon>0$ put
$y_{i,k}(\varepsilon)=y_i(1-k\varepsilon)$ for $k=0,\ldots,e_i-1$.  These $m$
values lie in $(0,1/2]$ and are distinct, and the groups stay separated.  For
each such $\varepsilon$ let $u^{(\varepsilon)}$ be the unique exponential sum
on these $m$ nodes with $u^{(\varepsilon)}_0=1$ and $u^{(\varepsilon)}_z=0$ for
$z\in Z$; existence and uniqueness are the Vandermonde argument opening this
section.  Theorem~\ref{thm:tail} applies with the same $f$ and gives
\[
  \Tail(u^{(\varepsilon)})
  \le\prod_{j=1}^{f+1}\frac{y_{(j)}(\varepsilon)}{1-y_{(j)}(\varepsilon)},
\]
where $y_{(1)}(\varepsilon)\le\cdots\le y_{(m)}(\varepsilon)$ is the sorted
perturbed multiset.

The functions $d\mapsto d^ky_i^d$ form an extended Chebyshev system, for which
confluent Hermite interpolation is unisolvent~\cite[Chapter~I,
Theorem~4.1]{karlin-studden}.  Hence, as the nodes coalesce,
$u^{(\varepsilon)}_d\to u_d$ for every fixed $d$.  Fatou's lemma for series,
applied to the nonnegative terms $|u^{(\varepsilon)}_d|$, gives
\[
  \Tail(u)=\sum_{d\ge1}|u_d|
  \le\liminf_{\varepsilon\to0}\Tail(u^{(\varepsilon)}).
\]
The right side is at most the limit of the displayed products, which is
$\prod_{j=1}^{f+1}y_{(j)}/(1-y_{(j)})$.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope}

Theorem~\ref{thm:mass} concerns normalized coefficient magnitudes, not
ordinary length or height.  Subject to $|f_D|\ge1$, it is uniform over the
degree of $F$ and all additional real or complex roots.  The contribution relative to
reduced-height duality is the displaced-zero comparison and its iteration;
the normalized determinant sequence and its consecutive-zero evaluation are
from Dubickas and Jankauskas~\cite{dj}.
