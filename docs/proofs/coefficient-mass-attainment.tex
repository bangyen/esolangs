\begin{abstract}
The certificate paper~\cite{coefficient-mass} proves that for real roots
$2\le r_1\le\cdots\le r_L$, repetitions allowed, and $F=\sum_jf_jx^j$ a
nonzero multiple of $\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)$, the $(u+1)$-st largest
nonleading coefficient magnitude satisfies
$b_{u+1}\ge|f_D|\prod_{i=u+1}^L(r_i-1)$, and that it is an infimum at
$(2,3)$ and $(2,3,5)$.  We show that the $(2,3,5)$ multiples are correctly
ordered at every degree, the $b_2$ family approaching its bound from above,
and identify a partial-sum criterion on the
surviving root product under which the same construction shows the bound
is an infimum in general.  For distinct roots the criterion is also
necessary: when it fails, the infimum lies strictly above the bound.  It is
then the reciprocal of the worst placement's best certificate tail, and
lifting certificates computes it at explicit root sets: at $(2,3,5,7)$ it is
$1704/31$.
The standing restriction $r_1\ge2$ cannot be dropped once $u\ge1$, already
at $L=2$; the node cutoff $y_i\le1/2$ underlying the tail comparison behind
the bound cannot be raised; and we extend that comparison to confluent
(repeated) nodes.  Beyond the cutoff we prove a repaired bound that keeps the
factors $\min(r_i-1,1)$ the original discards.  It holds for every $r_1>1$ and
is the infimum when the exempt roots lie in $(1,2]$ and the partial sums are
small, in particular for $b_L$ whenever $r_{L-1}\le2$.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Polynomial multiples; coefficient mass; reduced
height; Chebyshev systems; exponential sums.

\section{Introduction}

The certificate paper~\cite{coefficient-mass} proves a degree-independent
lower bound on the coefficient order statistics of a polynomial multiple.
For real roots $2\le r_1\le\cdots\le r_L$, repetitions allowed, and $F$ a
nonzero multiple of $\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)$ of degree $D\ge L$ with leading
coefficient $f_D$, its Theorem~3.2 gives, for every $0\le u\le L-1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:order}
  b_{u+1}\ge|f_D|\prod_{i=u+1}^L(r_i-1),
\end{equation}
where $b_k$ is the $k$th largest of the nonleading coefficient magnitudes
$|f_j|$, $j<D$, so that \eqref{eq:order} bounds $b_k$ with $k=u+1$; the case
$u=0$ needs only $r_1>1$.  The engine behind
\eqref{eq:order} is its Theorem~2.2, a comparison between the infinite
$\ell^1$ tail of a normalized exponential sum with prescribed zeros and the
tail of the sum with the same zero count but a consecutive zero set: for
nodes $0<y_1<\cdots<y_c\le1/2$, $y_i=\rho_i^{-1}$, and a zero set $Z$ of size
$c-1$ with leading run $f=\max\{0\le k\le c-1:\{1,\ldots,k\}\subseteq Z\}$,
the unique exponential sum $u$ on those nodes with $u_0=1$ and $u_z=0$
($z\in Z$) satisfies
\[
  \Tail(u)=\sum_{d\ge1}|u_d|\le\prod_{i=1}^{f+1}\frac{y_i}{1-y_i},
\]
with equality exactly at $Z=\{1,\ldots,c-1\}$; \eqref{eq:order} follows by
applying this with the excluded coefficient positions supplying $Z$.
Lemma~2.1 evaluates the consecutive case; Lemma~2.3 supplies the interlacing
step inside the comparison; Lemma~3.1 converts the tail bound into a
coefficient bound; Corollary~3.6 shows the resulting lower bound gives the
infimum of the logarithmic coefficient mass over monic multiples of the first
$L$ primes up to $O(L^2)$.  Throughout, an unqualified Lemma~2.1,
Theorem~2.2, Lemma~2.3, Lemma~3.1, Theorem~3.2 or Corollary~3.6 is the
result of that number in~\cite{coefficient-mass}; every other numbered result
is this paper's own.

Section~\ref{sec:extend} extends Theorem~2.2 to confluent nodes and shows
that neither the node cutoff $y_i\le1/2$ it rests on nor the standing
restriction $r_i\ge2$ in \eqref{eq:order} can be relaxed once $u\ge1$.  It
then proves a repaired bound, valid for every $r_1>1$, and shows it is sharp
when the exempt roots lie in $(1,2]$ and the partial sums are small.
Section~\ref{sec:attain} shows that the explicit monic multiples of
$(x-2)(x-3)(x-5)$ approaching \eqref{eq:order} are correctly ordered at every
degree, identifies the partial-sum criterion
behind the infimum in general, and proves that for distinct roots the bound
is an infimum exactly when the criterion holds.  Section~\ref{sec:value}
computes the infimum when it fails.  Section~\ref{sec:scope} records what is
proved and what remains open.

\section{Extensions and necessity of the hypotheses}\label{sec:extend}

\begin{proposition}[Confluent analogue]\label{prop:confluent}
Let $0<y_1<\cdots<y_c\le1/2$ and $e_1,\ldots,e_c\ge1$, put $m=e_1+\cdots+e_c$,
and let $y_{(1)}\le\cdots\le y_{(m)}$ be the multiset with each $y_i$ repeated
$e_i$ times.  Let
\[
  u_d=\sum_{i=1}^c P_i(d)y_i^d,\qquad \deg P_i<e_i,
\]
satisfy $u_0=1$ and $u_z=0$ for a set $Z$ of $m-1$ positive integers with
leading run $f$.  Then
\[
  \Tail(u)\le\prod_{j=1}^{f+1}\frac{y_{(j)}}{1-y_{(j)}}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The interpolation system is indexed by the Hermite basis, not by the
ordinary exponentials, and the two must be kept apart.  For
$\varepsilon>0$, $i=1,\ldots,c$ and $k=0,\ldots,e_i-1$ put
\[
  \phi_{i,k,\varepsilon}(d)
  =y_i^d\Bigl(\frac{1-e^{-\varepsilon d}}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)^k .
\]
For fixed $\varepsilon>0$, the binomial expansion gives
\[
  \phi_{i,k,\varepsilon}(d)
  =\frac1{\varepsilon^k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j\binom{k}{j}
    \bigl(y_ie^{-j\varepsilon}\bigr)^d,
\]
so each $\phi_{i,k,\varepsilon}$ is a finite linear combination of the
ordinary exponentials $d\mapsto z^d$ at the distinct nodes
$z_{i,j}=y_ie^{-j\varepsilon}$.  For $0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$ the $m$
nodes $z_{i,j}$ are distinct, lie in $(0,1/2]$, and stay separated between
distinct $i$.  Within one group $i$ the change of basis from
$\{z_{i,j}^{d}\}_{j<e_i}$ to
$\{\phi_{i,k,\varepsilon}\}_{k<e_i}$ is triangular with diagonal entries
$(-1)^k\varepsilon^{-k}$, hence invertible for $\varepsilon>0$, and the same
function space is spanned either way.

For such an $\varepsilon$ let $u^{(\varepsilon)}$ be the unique exponential
sum on the nodes $z_{i,j}$ with $u^{(\varepsilon)}_0=1$ and
$u^{(\varepsilon)}_z=0$ for $z\in Z$; existence and uniqueness are the
Vandermonde argument opening Section~2 of~\cite{coefficient-mass}.  Theorem~2.2 applies with
the same $f$ and gives
\[
  \Tail(u^{(\varepsilon)})
  \le\prod_{j=1}^{f+1}\frac{y_{(j)}(\varepsilon)}{1-y_{(j)}(\varepsilon)},
\]
where $y_{(1)}(\varepsilon)\le\cdots\le y_{(m)}(\varepsilon)$ is the sorted
perturbed multiset.

It remains to pass to the limit.  Write
$a^{(\varepsilon)}=(a^{(\varepsilon)}_{i,k})$ for the coefficients of
$u^{(\varepsilon)}$ in the $\phi$-basis; they solve
$M(\varepsilon)a^{(\varepsilon)}=e_0$, where $M(\varepsilon)$ is the
$m\times m$ matrix with rows indexed by $d\in\{0\}\cup Z$ and columns by
$(i,k)$, with entry $\phi_{i,k,\varepsilon}(d)$, and $e_0$ is the first
standard basis vector.  Each entry
$\phi_{i,k,\varepsilon}(d)=y_i^d\bigl((1-e^{-\varepsilon d})/\varepsilon\bigr)^k$
has a removable singularity at $\varepsilon=0$ and extends analytically there
with limit $d^ky_i^d$, so $M(\varepsilon)\to M(0)$ as $\varepsilon\to0$, where
$M(0)$ is the confluent matrix.  The functions
$d\mapsto d^ky_i^d$ form an extended Chebyshev system on $\R$: a nonzero
$\sum_iP_i(d)y_i^d$ with $\deg P_i<e_i$ has at most $m-1$ real
zeros~\cite[Part~Five, Ch.~1]{polya-szego}.  The
interpolation here is at the $m$ \emph{distinct} points $\{0\}\cup Z$---the
confluence sits in the basis, not in the nodes---so $M(0)$ is invertible by
ordinary unisolvence of a Chebyshev system at distinct
points~\cite[Chapter~I, Theorem~4.1]{karlin-studden}.  Matrix inversion is
continuous on the open set of invertible matrices, so
$a^{(\varepsilon)}\to M(0)^{-1}e_0$, and for every fixed $d$,
\[
  u^{(\varepsilon)}_d
  =\sum_{i,k}a^{(\varepsilon)}_{i,k}\phi_{i,k,\varepsilon}(d)
  \longrightarrow\sum_{i,k}a_{i,k}d^ky_i^d=u_d .
\]
Fatou's lemma for series, applied to the nonnegative terms
$|u^{(\varepsilon)}_d|$, gives
\[
  \Tail(u)=\sum_{d\ge1}|u_d|
  \le\liminf_{\varepsilon\to0}\Tail(u^{(\varepsilon)}).
\]
Finally the order statistics of a converging multiset converge, so the
product $\prod_{j=1}^{f+1}y_{(j)}(\varepsilon)/(1-y_{(j)}(\varepsilon))$
tends to $\prod_{j=1}^{f+1}y_{(j)}/(1-y_{(j)})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The node cutoff $1/2$ is sharp]\label{prop:cutoff}
No constant $a>1/2$ can replace $1/2$ in Theorem~2.2 or in
Proposition~\ref{prop:confluent}: for every $a\in(1/2,1)$ some configuration
with all nodes in $(0,a]$ violates the bound
$\Tail(u)\le\prod_{j=1}^{f+1}y_{(j)}/(1-y_{(j)})$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take a single node $a\in(1/2,1)$ of multiplicity $c$ and
$Z=\{2,3,\ldots,c\}$, so the sum is $u_d=a^d\prod_{k=2}^c(1-d/k)$.  Then
$u_0=1$, the prescribed zeros are exactly $Z$, the leading run is $f=0$, and
\[
  u_1=\frac ac,\qquad
  |u_d|=\frac1c\binom{d-2}{c-1}a^d\quad(d\ge c+1),
\]
so, summing the binomial series,
\[
  \Tail(u)=\frac ac+\frac{a^{c+1}}{c(1-a)^c}
  =\frac ac\Bigl[1+\Bigl(\frac a{1-a}\Bigr)^c\Bigr].
\]
The bound with $f=0$ would be $a/(1-a)$, but
\[
  \frac{\Tail(u)}{a/(1-a)}
  =\frac{1-a}{c}\Bigl[1+\Bigl(\frac a{1-a}\Bigr)^c\Bigr]\longrightarrow\infty
\]
because $a/(1-a)>1$, so $\Tail(u)>a/(1-a)$ for all large $c$.

To transfer the obstruction to distinct nodes, suppose the bound held for
every configuration with nodes in $(0,a]$.  Coalescing the $c$ nodes near $a$
as in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:confluent} gives sums
$u^{(\varepsilon)}$ on distinct nodes in $(0,a]$ that satisfy it, and
$u^{(\varepsilon)}_d\to u_d$ for each $d$.  By Fatou's lemma,
$\Tail(u)\le\liminf_{\varepsilon\to0}\Tail(u^{(\varepsilon)})\le a/(1-a)$,
contradicting the previous paragraph for large $c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The root restriction $r_1\ge2$ is necessary for $k\ge2$]
\label{prop:subtwo}
The $k=1$ case of \eqref{eq:order} holds for every root set $r_i>1$ by
Theorem~3.2, and for $L=1$ it is strict: every nonzero multiple
of $x-r$ has $b_1>|f_D|(r-1)$.  For $k\ge2$ the restriction $r_1\ge2$ cannot
be dropped, already at $L=2$, $k=2$, and already when only $r_1<2$: with
$r_1=3/2$ and $r_2=3$ there is a monic multiple $F$ of $(x-r_1)(x-r_2)$ of
degree $4$ with
\[
  b_2=\frac{81}{50}=1.62<2=r_2-1 .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $L=1$, $F(r)=0$ gives
$|f_D|r^D=\bigl|\sum_{j<D}f_jr^j\bigr|\le b_1(r^D-1)/(r-1)$, so
$b_1\ge|f_D|(r-1)r^D/(r^D-1)>|f_D|(r-1)$, with no use of $r\ge2$.

For the failure put $P=(x-\tfrac32)(x-3)=x^2+p_1x+p_0$ with
$p_1=-\tfrac92$, $p_0=\tfrac92$, and let $Q=x^2+q_1x+q_0$ be
monic, so that $F=PQ$ has
\[
  f_0=p_0q_0,\quad f_1=p_0q_1+p_1q_0,\quad f_2=p_0+p_1q_1+q_0,\quad
  f_3=p_1+q_1,\quad f_4=1 .
\]
Imposing $f_0=f_1=f_2$ is two linear equations in $(q_0,q_1)$: the first gives
$q_1=(p_0-p_1)q_0/p_0$, and substituting it into the second gives
\[
  q_0=\frac{p_0^2}{\delta},\qquad q_1=\frac{p_0(p_0-p_1)}{\delta},\qquad
  \delta=(p_0-p_1)^2+p_0(p_1-1),
\]
whence the common value is $f_0=f_1=f_2=p_0^3/\delta$.  Here
$\delta=\tfrac{225}4>0$, so
\[
  q_0=\frac9{25},\qquad q_1=\frac{18}{25},\qquad
  f_0=f_1=f_2=\frac{81}{50}=1.62,
\]
while $f_3=p_1+q_1=-\tfrac{189}{50}=-3.78$, that is,
$F=x^4-\tfrac{189}{50}x^3+\tfrac{81}{50}x^2+\tfrac{81}{50}x+\tfrac{81}{50}$.
The four nonleading magnitudes are $|f_3|$ and three copies of
$p_0^3/\delta$, so $b_2=p_0^3/\delta<2=r_2-1$, which is what \eqref{eq:order}
would assert for $u=1$; the second root obeys $r_2\ge2$, so only $r_1$ is out
of range.
\end{proof}

The restriction belongs to the product, though, not to the roots.  Lemma~3.1
discards, for each exempt position, a factor $y_i/(1-y_i)$ once it is at most
$1$, and that factor exceeds $1$ precisely when $r_i<2$.  Keeping it gives a
bound that holds for every $r_1>1$.

\begin{theorem}[Repaired tail comparison]\label{thm:repaired}
Let $0<y_1<\cdots<y_c<1$, and let $Z$, $f$ and $u$ be as in Theorem~2.2.
Then
\[
  \Tail(u)\le\prod_{i=1}^{f+1}\frac{y_i}{1-y_i}
  \prod_{i=f+2}^{c}\max\Bigl(1,\frac{y_i}{1-y_i}\Bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}

Within the cutoff every factor of the second product is $1$, and this is
Theorem~2.2.

\begin{proof}
Run Theorem~2.2's induction on the number $c-1-f$ of displaced zeros.  The
base case is Lemma~2.1, which needs only $y_i<1$.  In the displaced step, with
$z=\max Z\ge1$ and $F$, $G$, $\mu=|F_z|$ as there, the splitting identity
\[
  \Tail(F)-\Tail(u)=\mu\bigl(2\Gamma_F(z)-\Gamma_G(z)+A_G\bigr),
  \qquad
  \Gamma_V(z)=\frac{\sum_{d\ge z}|V_d|}{|V_z|},
  \quad A_G=\sum_{1\le d<z}|G_d|\ge0,
\]
rests on the zero bound alone, and the first half
$\Gamma_G(z)\le\Gamma_F(z)/(1-y_c)$ of the correction-ratio bound rests on
Lemma~2.3, which needs no upper bound on the nodes, and on a geometric series
in $y_c<1$.  Since $\mu\Gamma_F(z)=\sum_{d\ge z}|F_d|$, together they give
\[
  \Tail(u)\le\Tail(F)+\frac{2y_c-1}{1-y_c}\sum_{d\ge z}|F_d| .
\]
If $y_c\le1/2$ the correction is at most $0$.  Otherwise it is at most
$\frac{2y_c-1}{1-y_c}\Tail(F)$, and $1+\frac{2y_c-1}{1-y_c}=\frac{y_c}{1-y_c}$.
Either way
\[
  \Tail(u)\le\max\Bigl(1,\frac{y_c}{1-y_c}\Bigr)\Tail(F).
\]
The sum $F$ lives on $y_1,\ldots,y_{c-1}$ and its zero set has the same leading
run $f$, so the induction hypothesis bounds $\Tail(F)$ by the product over
those nodes, and the factor for $y_c$ completes the product.
\end{proof}

The deletion step itself can reverse beyond the cutoff: on nodes
$(1/3,1/2,9/10)$ with $Z=\{1,3\}$ it takes $\Tail$ from $2.077$ down to $0.500$.
The factor $\max(1,y_c/(1-y_c))=9$ absorbs the reversal.  The limit argument
of Proposition~\ref{prop:confluent} carries the theorem to confluent nodes
unchanged.  At Proposition~\ref{prop:cutoff}'s witness, one node $a$ of
multiplicity $c$ with $f=0$, it gives $(a/(1-a))^c$ against
$\Tail(u)=(a/c)[1+(a/(1-a))^c]$, a ratio of at most $1/c$.

\begin{corollary}[Repaired order statistics]\label{cor:repaired}
Let $r_1\le\cdots\le r_L$ be real with $r_1>1$, repetitions allowed, and let
$F$ be a nonzero multiple of $\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)$.  Then for every
$0\le u\le L-1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:repaired}
  b_{u+1}\ge|f_D|\prod_{i=u+1}^L(r_i-1)\prod_{i=1}^{u}\min(r_i-1,1),
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:order} when $r_u\ge2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Follow Lemma~3.1's proof with Theorem~\ref{thm:repaired} in place of
Theorem~2.2.  The zero set there has leading run $f\ge L-u-1$.  The $L-u$
smallest nodes are the reciprocals of $r_{u+1},\ldots,r_L$ and contribute
$\prod_{i>u}(r_i-1)^{-1}$.  Every other node $1/r_i$, $i\le u$, contributes
either $y_i/(1-y_i)$ or $\max(1,y_i/(1-y_i))$, and both are at most
$1/\min(r_i-1,1)$.  Repeated roots and a general $f_D$ are handled as in
Theorem~3.2's proof.  Its perturbation $r_i+i\varepsilon$ keeps every root
above $1$, and the right side of \eqref{eq:repaired} is continuous in the
roots.
\end{proof}

At $(\tfrac32,3)$ the corollary gives $b_2\ge1$, below the $1.62$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:subtwo}, and $1$ is the infimum.  More generally the
repaired bound is attained whenever the $u$ exempt roots lie in $(1,2]$ and
the partial sums of the whole root product are small enough.  The multiples
that attain it put the exempt coefficients at the top rather than the
bottom.  Here $S_j(P)$ are the partial sums of \eqref{eq:wgen} with $u=0$.

\begin{proposition}[Sharpness below $2$]\label{prop:subtwosharp}
Let $r_1\le\cdots\le r_L$ be real with $r_1>1$, repetitions allowed, put
$P=\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)$, and let $0\le u\le L-1$.  Let $\sigma$ be the
$(u+1)$-st largest of $|S_1(P)|,\ldots,|S_{L-1}(P)|$, or $0$ if $u=L-1$.  Then
\[
  \inf_Qb_{u+1}(QP)\le\max\bigl(|P(1)|,\sigma\bigr)
\]
over monic $Q\in\R[x]$.  If $r_u\le2$ and $\sigma\le|P(1)|$, the infimum is
$|P(1)|$, which is the value of \eqref{eq:repaired}.  In particular
$\inf_Qb_L(QP)=|P(1)|$ whenever $r_{L-1}\le2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $P(0)\ne0$, $x$ is invertible modulo $P$.  For $n\ge1$ let $R_n$ be the
polynomial of degree less than $L$ with $x^nR_n\equiv1\pmod P$.  Its
coefficient vector is $T^n$ applied to that of $1$, where $T$ is
multiplication by $x^{-1}$ on $\R[x]/(P)$.  The eigenvalues of $T$ are the
$1/r_i<1$, so $R_n\to0$, and for large $n$ we have $R_n(1)\ne1$.  Put
\[
  t_n=\frac{P(1)}{1-R_n(1)},\qquad M_n=P+t_nR_n .
\]
Then $M_n$ is monic of degree $L$ with $M_n(1)=t_n$, so
$M_n=(x-1)\widetilde A_n+t_n$ with $\widetilde A_n$ monic of degree $L-1$.
Moreover $x^nM_n-t_n\equiv x^nP+t_n(x^nR_n-1)\equiv0\pmod P$, and
$x^nM_n-t_n$ vanishes at $1$, where $P$ does not, so $P$ divides
\[
  F_n=\frac{x^nM_n-t_n}{x-1}=x^n\widetilde A_n+t_n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x^j .
\]
Thus $F_n$ is a monic multiple of $P$.  Its nonleading coefficients are the
$L-1$ lower coefficients of $\widetilde A_n$, followed by $t_n$ repeated $n$
times.  At most $u$ of those can exceed the larger of $|t_n|$ and the
$(u+1)$-st largest lower coefficient of $\widetilde A_n$, so $b_{u+1}(F_n)$ is
at most that larger value.  As $n\to\infty$, $t_n\to P(1)$ and
$\widetilde A_n\to(P-P(1))/(x-1)$.  By \eqref{eq:wgen} the lower coefficients
of that limit are $S_1(P),\ldots,S_{L-1}(P)$, which proves the bound.  If
$r_u\le2$, every $\min(r_i-1,1)$ with $i\le u$ is $r_i-1$, so the right side
of \eqref{eq:repaired} is $|P(1)|$.  At $u=L-1$, $\sigma=0$.
\end{proof}

At $(\tfrac32,10,20)$ with $u=1$, for example, the partial sums are
$1,-30.5,214.5,-85.5$, so $\sigma=30.5$ and the infimum of $b_2$ is
$85.5=|P(1)|$.  The stated product $171$ is false there.

\section{Sharpness at explicit root sets}\label{sec:attain}

At roots $(2,3)$ the certificate paper already settles the question: it
exhibits monic multiples
$F_n=x^{n+1}-a_nx^n+t_n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x^j$ of $(x-2)(x-3)$ with
$b_2(F_n)=t_n$ decreasing to $2$~\cite[Proposition~4.5]{coefficient-mass}, so
the $u=1$ bound is an infimum there, approached but never attained.

The same construction reaches $L=3$.  Write the multiple from the top: one
leading $1$, a short free prefix, a long run of a single value $t$, and $u$
exempt coefficients at the bottom.  The exempt coefficients grow like
$r_u^D$ and so survive division by $r^{D-1}$ only at the $u$ smallest roots,
which they absorb; the run must therefore satisfy the remaining $L-u$ roots
alone, and its value is what \eqref{eq:order} predicts.  The certificate
paper proves the two limits below~\cite[Section~4]{coefficient-mass}; what
is added here is that the ordering holds exactly at every degree, with
$t_D>a_D+1>8$ throughout, and the Descartes step that the general
construction later reuses.

\begin{proposition}[The bounds at roots $(2,3,5)$ are infima]
\label{prop:sharp235}
For $D\ge5$ let $(a_D,t_D,c_D)$ be the solution of the linear system
$F_D(2)=F_D(3)=F_D(5)=0$ in
\[
  F_D(x)=x^D-a_Dx^{D-1}+t_D\sum_{j=1}^{D-2}x^j+c_D ,
\]
and for $D\ge6$ let $(s_D,e_D,e_D')$ be the solution of
$G_D(2)=G_D(3)=G_D(5)=0$ in
\[
  G_D(x)=x^D-s_D\sum_{j=2}^{D-1}x^j+e_Dx+e_D' .
\]
Both systems are nonsingular, $F_D$ and $G_D$ are monic multiples of
$(x-2)(x-3)(x-5)$ with $b_2(F_D)=t_D$ and $b_3(G_D)=s_D$, and $t_D\to8$,
$s_D\to4$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp235}
  \inf_Q b_2\bigl(QP\bigr)=8=(r_2-1)(r_3-1),
  \qquad
  \inf_Q b_3\bigl(QP\bigr)=4=r_3-1,
\end{equation}
over monic $Q\in\R[x]$, with $P(x)=(x-2)(x-3)(x-5)$: the values
\eqref{eq:order} asserts for $u=1$ and $u=2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both systems are nonsingular for every $D$ in range.  A kernel vector of the
first is an $H=-ax^{D-1}+t\sum_{j=1}^{D-2}x^j+c$ vanishing at $2,3,5$, and
\[
  (x-1)H=-ax^D+(a+t)x^{D-1}+(c-t)x-c
\]
has four terms, hence at most three positive roots by Descartes' rule; but
$1,2,3,5$ are four, so $H\equiv0$ and $a=t=c=0$.  The second is the same with
$(x-1)H=-sx^D+(s+e)x^2+(e'-e)x-e'$.

Divide $F_D(r)=0$ by $r^{D-1}$ and sum the geometric progression:
\[
  (r-a_D)+\frac{t_D}{r-1}
  +r^{1-D}\Bigl(c_D-\frac{r\,t_D}{r-1}\Bigr)=0,
  \qquad r\in\{2,3,5\}.
\]
Put $\gamma_D=2^{1-D}c_D$, so that $r^{1-D}c_D=\gamma_D(2/r)^{D-1}$.  In the
unknowns $(a_D,t_D,\gamma_D)$ the three equations read
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    -a+t\bigl(1-2^{2-D}\bigr)+\gamma&=-2,\\
    -a+t\bigl(\tfrac12-\tfrac323^{1-D}\bigr)+\gamma(2/3)^{D-1}&=-3,\\
    -a+t\bigl(\tfrac14-\tfrac545^{1-D}\bigr)+\gamma(2/5)^{D-1}&=-5 .
  \end{aligned}
\]
Every $D$-dependent entry is $O((2/3)^{D})$, so the coefficient matrix tends
to
\[
  M=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&1\\-1&\tfrac12&0\\-1&\tfrac14&0\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad \det M=\tfrac14\ne0 .
\]
Its inverse therefore converges to
$M^{-1}$, so $(a_D,t_D,\gamma_D)$ converges to the solution of $M$ against
$(-2,-3,-5)$.  The last two rows of that limit give
$-a+\tfrac12t=-3$ and $-a+\tfrac14t=-5$, whence $t=8$ and $a=7$; the first
row then gives $\gamma=-3$, so $c_D\sim-3\cdot2^{D-1}$.

The ordering is exact for every $D$, not only in the limit.  $F_D$ has the
three positive roots $2,3,5$, and its coefficient sequence
$(1,-a_D,t_D,\ldots,t_D,c_D)$ offers three sign changes only if $a_D>0$,
$t_D>0$ and $c_D<0$, a vanishing entry leaving at most two; so Descartes
forces all three signs.  Multiplying the displayed equation at $r$ by $r-1$
gives $(r-1)(r-a_D)+t_D=\varepsilon_r$ with
$\varepsilon_r=r^{1-D}\bigl(r\,t_D+(r-1)|c_D|\bigr)>0$, and the rows $r=3,5$
solve to
\[
  a_D=7+\tfrac12(\varepsilon_3-\varepsilon_5),\qquad
  t_D=8+2\varepsilon_3-\varepsilon_5,\qquad
  t_D-a_D=1+\tfrac32\varepsilon_3-\tfrac12\varepsilon_5 ,
\]
where
$\varepsilon_5/\varepsilon_3=(3/5)^{D-1}(5t_D+4|c_D|)/(3t_D+2|c_D|)
<2(3/5)^{D-1}<1$.  Hence $t_D>a_D+1>8$: both limits are approached from
above.  The nonleading magnitudes are $a_D$ once, $t_D$ exactly $D-2\ge3$
times and $|c_D|$ once, and the run already exceeds $a_D$, so at most one
entry exceeds $t_D$ and $b_2(F_D)=t_D$.  Theorem~3.2 with $u=1$
gives $b_2\ge8$ for every monic multiple, and $t_D\to8$.

For $G_D$ the two exempt coefficients grow like $3^D$ and must absorb both
$2$ and $3$, so the rescaling is the one that keeps the equation at $r=2$
finite: put
\[
  E_D=3^{2-D}e_D,
  \qquad
  \sigma_D=2^{1-D}\bigl(2e_D+e_D'\bigr).
\]
Dividing $G_D(r)=0$ by $r^{D-1}$ and substituting, the exempt terms
contribute $\sigma_D$ at $r=2$ by definition, $E_D/3+\sigma_D(2/3)^{D-1}$ at
$r=3$, and $O((3/5)^D)$ at $r=5$.  In the unknowns $(s_D,E_D,\sigma_D)$ the
system therefore reads
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    -2s\bigl(1-2^{2-D}\bigr)+\sigma&=-2,\\
    -\tfrac32s\bigl(1-3^{2-D}\bigr)+\tfrac13E+\sigma(2/3)^{D-1}&=-3,\\
    -\tfrac54s\bigl(1-5^{2-D}\bigr)+O\bigl((3/5)^D\bigr)&=-5,
  \end{aligned}
\]
whose coefficient matrix tends to
\[
  N=\begin{pmatrix}-2&0&1\\-\tfrac32&\tfrac13&0\\-\tfrac54&0&0\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad \det N=\tfrac5{12}\ne0 .
\]
Every $D$-dependent entry is $O((2/3)^{D})$, so as before the solution
converges to that of $N$ against $(-2,-3,-5)$, namely
$(s,E,\sigma)=(4,9,6)$; the last row alone forces $s=4$.  The nonleading
magnitudes of $G_D$ are $s_D$ exactly $D-2$ times and $|e_D|,|e_D'|$ once
each, and the same sign count applies: $(1,-s_D,\ldots,-s_D,e_D,e_D')$ offers
three changes only if $s_D>0$, so $s_D>0$ for every $D$.  The run then
occupies $D-2\ge3$ positions, and whatever the two exempt magnitudes do the
third largest is $s_D$, so $b_3(G_D)=s_D$.
Theorem~3.2 with $u=2$ gives $b_3\ge4$, and
$s_D\to4$, which completes \eqref{eq:sharp235}.
\end{proof}

Neither family was guessed.  Write $P_u(x)=\prod_{i>u}(x-r_i)$ for the
surviving roots, $m=L-u$, and let $\widetilde P_u(y)=y^mP_u(1/y)$ be its
reverse.  In the reversed variables the limit construction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wgen}
  W(y)=\sum_{d\ge0}w_dy^d=\frac{\widetilde P_u(y)}{1-y},
\end{equation}
which vanishes at $y=1/r_i$ for every surviving root because
$\widetilde P_u$ does.  Its coefficients are therefore the partial sums
$S_j=c_m+c_{m-1}+\cdots+c_{m-j}$ of the coefficients $c_k$ of $P_u$ read down
from the leading $c_m=1$, and they stabilize at
$S_m=P_u(1)=(-1)^m\prod_{i>u}(r_i-1)$ once $j\ge m$.  So the free prefix is
$S_1,\ldots,S_{m-1}$ and the run value is $|S_m|$, which is exactly
\eqref{eq:order}: the construction reproduces the bound because the sum of the
coefficients of $P_u$ is $P_u(1)$.

This makes the requirement explicit.  At $u=0$ both the criterion and this
series are already in Dubickas and Jankauskas: their Corollary~7 gives
$H(P)=|P(1)|$ under the same partial-sum condition, together with the one-sign
proviso, and exhibits $\widetilde P/(1-y)$ as the extremal
series~\cite[Corollary~7]{dj}.  What is added here is the passage to $u\ge1$,
where the $u$ exempt coefficients absorb the $u$ smallest roots.  The run is
the $(u+1)$-st largest magnitude only if no partial sum exceeds the total,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial}
  |S_j|\le|S_m|\qquad(1\le j<m),
\end{equation}
and \eqref{eq:partial} can fail.

At $(2,3,5,7)$ with $u=1$ the coefficients
of $P_1$ are $1,-15,71,-105$, with partial sums $1,-14,57,-48$; since
$57>48$, \eqref{eq:partial} fails.  The same happens at $(2,3,4,5)$, sums
$1,-11,36,-24$, and at $(2,3,5,6)$, sums $1,-13,50,-40$.  At $(3,4,5,6)$, by
contrast, the sums are $1,-14,60,-60$, so \eqref{eq:partial} holds with
equality.

\begin{proposition}[Partial-sum criterion]\label{prop:partial}
Let $2\le r_1<\cdots<r_L$ be distinct and $0\le u\le L-1$.  If
\eqref{eq:partial} holds, then
$\inf_Qb_{u+1}\bigl(Q\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)\bigr)=\prod_{i=u+1}^L(r_i-1)$ over
monic $Q\in\R[x]$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~3.2 gives $\ge$.  For the other direction put $m=L-u$ and write
$F_D=x^D+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}a_kx^{D-k}+t\sum_{j=u}^{D-m}x^j+C$ with $\deg C<u$,
the $L$ unknowns fixed by $F_D(r_i)=0$.  The Descartes step of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp235} carries over, $(x-1)$ times a kernel element
having $L+1$ terms against the $L+1$ positive roots $1,r_1,\ldots,r_L$.
Divide by $r_i^D$, sum the run, and trade the exempt coefficients for their
values $\sigma_i=r_i^{-D}C(r_i)$, a Vandermonde change of variables.  The
limit system is block triangular, and its surviving rows make the monic
$(x-1)\widetilde A+t$ of degree $m$, where
$\widetilde A=x^{m-1}+a_1x^{m-2}+\cdots+a_{m-1}$, vanish at the $m$ surviving
roots.  So it is $P_u$, and
\[
  t=P_u(1),\qquad
  \widetilde A=\frac{P_u-P_u(1)}{x-1},\qquad
  \sigma_i=-\frac{r_i^{1-m}P_u(r_i)}{r_i-1}\ne0 ,
\]
the coefficients of $\widetilde A$ being the prefix $S_1,\ldots,S_{m-1}$ of
\eqref{eq:wgen}.  The perturbation is $O((r_u/r_{u+1})^D)$ and every exempt
coefficient grows like $r_u^D$, so for large $D$ those are the $u$ largest and
$b_{u+1}\to\max_j|S_j|$, which is $|S_m|$ exactly when \eqref{eq:partial}
holds.  Distinctness enters three times---the Descartes step, the Vandermonde
change, and the surviving block---and $r_u<r_{u+1}$ is what kills the cross
terms and makes the exempt coefficients diverge.
\end{proof}

The conclusion outlives it, though.  At $(r,r)$ the
confluent member $F_D=x^D-t_D\sum_{j=1}^{D-1}x^j+c_D$ fixed by
$F_D(r)=F_D'(r)=0$ has $b_2(F_D)=t_D$, the run occupying $D-1\ge2$ positions,
and at $r=3$
\[
  t_D=\frac{4D\cdot3^{D-1}}{3^{D-1}(2D-3)+1}\longrightarrow2=r_2-1,
\]
so \eqref{eq:order} is an infimum there too, approached at rate $1/D$ rather
than geometrically.

For distinct roots the criterion is also necessary.  The argument runs on
the dual side of Lemma~3.1.  Put $y_i=1/r_i$, so that
$1/2\ge y_1>\cdots>y_L>0$, and call an exponential sum
$v_d=\sum_i\lambda_iy_i^d$ on some of these nodes a \emph{certificate for}
$X\subset\N_{>0}$ if $v_0=1$ and $v_w=0$ for $w\in X$.  If $F$ is a monic
multiple of $\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)$ of degree $D$ and $X$ is the set of
distances $D-j$ of $u$ positions $j$ carrying its $u$ largest nonleading
magnitudes, the identity in the proof of Lemma~3.1 reads
$0=1+\sum_{j<D}f_jv_{D-j}$, the terms with $D-j\in X$ vanish, and every other
$|f_j|$ is at most $b_{u+1}(F)$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak}
  1\le b_{u+1}(F)\,\Tail(v)
\end{equation}
for every certificate $v$ for $X$.  Write
$B=\prod_{i=u+1}^L(r_i-1)^{-1}$.  It therefore suffices to find a
$\delta>0$ such that every $X$ of size $u$ has a certificate with
$\Tail(v)\le B-\delta$.  Three lemmas supply the pieces: the partial sums
alternate in sign, a failing partial sum improves the consecutive
certificate, and zeros far from the origin cost only the largest nodes.

\begin{lemma}[Signs of the partial sums]\label{lem:signs}
Let $r_1,\ldots,r_m>1$, let $S_0,\ldots,S_m$ be the partial sums of the
coefficients of $\prod_{i=1}^m(x-r_i)$ read down from the leading one, and put
$s_i=r_i-1$.  Then
\[
  (-1)^jS_j=\sum_{k=0}^{j}e_k(s)\binom{m-1-k}{j-k}>0\quad(0\le j\le m-1),
  \qquad (-1)^mS_m=e_m(s),
\]
where $e_k$ is the elementary symmetric polynomial.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:wgen}, $S_j$ is the coefficient of $y^j$ in
$\prod_i(1-r_iy)/(1-y)$, so $(-1)^jS_j$ is the coefficient of $y^j$ in
$\prod_i(1+r_iy)/(1+y)$.  Writing $1+r_iy=(1+y)+s_iy$ and grouping the
expansion by the number of factors $s_iy$ chosen,
\[
  \frac{\prod_i(1+r_iy)}{1+y}
  =\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}e_k(s)\,y^k(1+y)^{m-1-k}+e_m(s)\frac{y^m}{1+y}.
\]
The terms with $k<m$ are polynomials of degree $m-1$ and contribute
$e_k(s)\binom{m-1-k}{j-k}$ to $y^j$; the last contributes nothing below $y^m$
and $e_m(s)$ at $y^m$.  The $k=0$ term alone gives $\binom{m-1}{j}\ge1$.
\end{proof}

In particular \eqref{eq:partial} reads
$\sum_{k\le j}e_k(s)\binom{m-1-k}{j-k}\le e_m(s)$ for $1\le j<m$, with
$s_i=r_i-1$ over the surviving roots; at $(3,5,7)$, $s=(2,4,6)$, the two sums
are $14$ and $57$ against $e_3(s)=48$.

\begin{lemma}[Improving the consecutive certificate]\label{lem:improve}
Let $\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_n>1$ be distinct, $Q=\prod_{i=1}^n(x-\rho_i)$ with
partial sums $S_j(Q)$, and $v^*$ the certificate on the nodes $1/\rho_i$ with
zero set $\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$, so that $\Tail(v^*)=1/|Q(1)|$ by Lemma~2.1.  If
$X_0\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$ and some $j\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\setminus X_0$
has $|S_j(Q)|>|Q(1)|$, then some certificate for $X_0$ on the same nodes has
tail less than $1/|Q(1)|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\delta$ be the exponential sum on the nodes with $\delta_0=0$ and
$\delta_i=[i=j]$ for $1\le i\le n-1$; it exists by the Vandermonde argument.
With $\Pi(x)=\prod_i(1-x/\rho_i)=\sum_s\pi_sx^s$, its generating function is
$N(x)/\Pi(x)$ with $\deg N\le n-1$, and $N$ is the truncation of
$\Pi\sum_d\delta_dx^d$ below degree $n$, namely
$N(x)=\sum_{t=j}^{n-1}\pi_{t-j}x^t$.  Since $\Pi$ is $Q$ times the constant
$\prod_i(-1/\rho_i)$, evaluating at $x=1$ gives
\[
  \sum_{d\ge n}\delta_d=\frac{N(1)}{\Pi(1)}-1
  =-\frac{\pi_{n-j}+\cdots+\pi_n}{\Pi(1)}
  =-\frac{S_j(Q)}{Q(1)} .
\]
By Lemma~2.1, $v^*_d$ has the constant sign $\sigma=(-1)^{n+1}$ for $d\ge n$
and $|v^*_d|\ge\bigl(\prod_iy_i\bigr)y_{\max}^{\,d-n}$, while
$|\delta_d|\le Cy_{\max}^{\,d}$; so for $0<\tau$ small enough
$v=v^*+t\delta$ with $t=\tau\sigma\sgn\bigl(S_j(Q)/Q(1)\bigr)$ keeps the sign
$\sigma$ at every $d\ge n$.  It is a certificate for $X_0$, since it vanishes
on $\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\setminus\{j\}$, and
\[
  \Tail(v)=\tau+\sigma\sum_{d\ge n}\bigl(v^*_d+t\delta_d\bigr)
  =\Tail(v^*)-\tau\Bigl(\Bigl|\frac{S_j(Q)}{Q(1)}\Bigr|-1\Bigr)
  <\Tail(v^*).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Far zeros]\label{lem:far}
Let $1>y_1>\cdots>y_L>0$ and $0\le k<L$.  Let $v^\infty$ be a certificate for
a finite set $X_0$ on $y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$, and let $E_\nu\subset\N_{>0}$,
$\nu\ge1$, be sets of size $k$ with $\min E_\nu\to\infty$.  Then for large
$\nu$ there are certificates $v^{(\nu)}$ for $X_0\cup E_\nu$ on
$y_1,\ldots,y_L$ with $\Tail(v^{(\nu)}-v^\infty)\to0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $k=0$ take $v^{(\nu)}=v^\infty$.  Put $n=L-k$ and $s=n-1-|X_0|$.  The
certificates for $X_0$ on $y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$ form an affine space $\mathcal
A$ of dimension $s$.  Fix $s+1$ positive integers $q_0,\ldots,q_s$ outside
$X_0$ and let $v^{[i]}\in\mathcal A$ also vanish at every $q_l$, $l\ne i$.
Evaluation at $q_0,\ldots,q_s$ maps $\mathcal A$ injectively onto an
$s$-dimensional affine subspace of $\R^{s+1}$ missing the origin, by the zero
bound, and sends $v^{[i]}$ to a nonzero multiple of the $i$th unit vector.
These $s+1$ images are affinely independent, so $v^\infty$ is an affine
combination of the $v^{[i]}$, and it suffices to treat each $v^{[i]}$.  We may
thus assume that $v^\infty$ has a zero set $Z_0\supseteq X_0$ of size $n-1$,
disjoint from $E_\nu$ for large $\nu$.

Let $v^{(\nu)}$ be the certificate on all $L$ nodes with zero set
$Z_0\cup E_\nu$.  Order the points $0$, then $Z_0$, then
$E_\nu=\{e_1<\cdots<e_k\}$, and let $A$ be the matrix $(y_j^p)$ with rows
indexed by these points; by Cramer's rule $v^{(\nu)}_d=\det A(d)/\det A$,
where $A(d)$ has the row of $0$ replaced by $(y_j^d)_j$.  Expanding both
along the rows of $E_\nu$,
\[
  \det A(d)=\sum_{|J|=k}\varepsilon_J\,\alpha_J\,\beta_J(d),
  \qquad
  \alpha_J=\det\bigl(y_j^{e}\bigr)_{e\in E_\nu,\,j\in J},
  \quad
  \beta_J(d)=\det\bigl(y_j^{p}\bigr)_{p\in\{d\}\cup Z_0,\,j\notin J},
\]
with signs $\varepsilon_J$ independent of $d$ and $\nu$.  For
$J=\{j_1<\cdots<j_k\}$ every term of $\alpha_J$ is a product
$\prod_ty_{j_t}^{e_{\pi(t)}}$, the largest being
$M_J=\prod_ty_{j_t}^{e_{k+1-t}}$, so $|\alpha_J|\le k!\,M_J$.  For
$J^*=\{1,\ldots,k\}$, $\alpha_{J^*}$ is an alternant, the Vandermonde product
$\prod_{a<b\le k}(y_a-y_b)$ times a Schur polynomial in $y_1,\ldots,y_k$ with
nonnegative coefficients that contains the monomial $M_{J^*}/\prod_{t\le
k}y_t^{k-t}$; hence $|\alpha_{J^*}|\ge cM_{J^*}$ with
$c=\prod_{a<b\le k}(1-y_b/y_a)>0$.  If $J\ne J^*$ then $y_{j_t}\le y_t$ for
every $t$, and $j_t\ge k+1$ for some $t$, so
$M_J\le(y_{k+1}/y_k)^{e_1}M_{J^*}$ and
\[
  \eta_\nu=\max_{J\ne J^*}\Bigl|\frac{\alpha_J}{\alpha_{J^*}}\Bigr|
  \le\frac{k!}{c}\Bigl(\frac{y_{k+1}}{y_k}\Bigr)^{e_1}\longrightarrow0 .
\]
The minors $\beta_J(d)$ do not depend on $\nu$, $\beta_{J^*}(0)\ne0$ by the
zero bound, $v^\infty_d=\beta_{J^*}(d)/\beta_{J^*}(0)$ by Cramer's rule on
$y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$, and expanding along the row of $d$ gives
$\Sigma_J=\sum_{d\ge0}|\beta_J(d)|<\infty$.  Dividing numerator and
denominator by $\varepsilon_{J^*}\alpha_{J^*}$,
\[
  v^{(\nu)}_d-v^\infty_d
  =\frac{\rho_\nu(d)-v^\infty_d\,\rho_\nu(0)}{\beta_{J^*}(0)+\rho_\nu(0)},
  \qquad |\rho_\nu(d)|\le\eta_\nu\sum_{J\ne J^*}|\beta_J(d)|,
\]
and summing over $d\ge1$,
$\Tail(v^{(\nu)}-v^\infty)\le\eta_\nu\bigl(1+\Tail(v^\infty)\bigr)
\sum_{J\ne J^*}\Sigma_J\big/\bigl(|\beta_{J^*}(0)|-|\rho_\nu(0)|\bigr)\to0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Necessity of the partial-sum criterion]\label{thm:converse}
Let $2\le r_1<\cdots<r_L$ be distinct and $0\le u\le L-1$.  If
\eqref{eq:partial} fails, then
\[
  \inf_Qb_{u+1}\Bigl(Q\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)\Bigr)>\prod_{i=u+1}^L(r_i-1)
\]
over monic $Q\in\R[x]$.  With Proposition~\ref{prop:partial},
\eqref{eq:order} is the infimum exactly when \eqref{eq:partial} holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:weak} it suffices to find $\delta>0$ such that every
$X\subset\N_{>0}$ of size $u$ has a certificate on $y_1,\ldots,y_L$ with
$\Tail\le B-\delta$.  Suppose not, and take $X_\nu$ whose certificates all
have $\Tail\ge B-1/\nu$.  Passing to a subsequence, each of the $u$ elements
of $X_\nu$, in increasing order, is eventually constant or tends to infinity,
so $X_\nu=X_0\cup E_\nu$ with $X_0$ fixed, $|E_\nu|=k$ and
$\min E_\nu\to\infty$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:far}, a certificate $v^\infty$ for
$X_0$ on $y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$ with $\Tail(v^\infty)<B$ yields certificates
for $X_\nu$ with tails tending to $\Tail(v^\infty)$, a contradiction.  So it
suffices to produce one.  Put $n=L-k$ and $u_0=|X_0|=u-k$.

If $u_0=0$ the nodes are the reciprocals of the surviving roots, and since
\eqref{eq:partial} fails, Lemma~\ref{lem:improve} with $Q=P_u$ and
$X_0=\varnothing$ gives $\Tail(v^\infty)<1/|P_u(1)|=B$.

If $u_0\ge1$, take the certificate of Lemma~3.1's proof: zero set $Z$
consisting of $X_0$ and the first $m-1$ positive integers outside it, so
$|Z|=n-1$ and the leading run of $Z$ is $f\ge m-1$.  Theorem~2.2 on
$y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L\le1/2$ bounds $\Tail$ by the product of $1/(r_i-1)$ over
the $f+1$ largest of $r_{k+1},\ldots,r_L$, with equality only if
$Z=\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$.  Every factor is at most $1$ and $f+1\ge m$, so
$\Tail\le B$, strictly unless $Z=\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$ and the factors
$1/(r_i-1)$, $k<i\le u$, all equal $1$.  The last includes $i=u$, and
$r_u=2$ forces $u=1$ and $r_1=2$, hence $k=0$ and $X_0=\{w\}$ with
$w\le L-1$.

In that case $r_1-1=1$, so $1/|P(1)|=B$ for $P=\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:improve} with $Q=P$ and $X_0=\{w\}$ needs one
$j\in\{1,\ldots,L-1\}\setminus\{w\}$ with $|S_j(P)|>|P(1)|$.  Since
$P=(x-2)P_1$, $S_j(P)=S_j(P_1)-2S_{j-1}(P_1)$, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:signs} consecutive partial sums of $P_1$ have opposite signs,
so $|S_j(P)|=|S_j(P_1)|+2|S_{j-1}(P_1)|$ for $1\le j\le m=L-1$.  As
\eqref{eq:partial} fails, $|S_i(P_1)|>|P_1(1)|=|P(1)|$ for some $1\le i<m$,
and then both $j=i$ and $j=i+1$ qualify; one of them is not $w$.
\end{proof}

The proof also shows how a multiple can approach \eqref{eq:order}.  Outside
the case $u=1$, $r_1=2$, every placement with an exempt coefficient at
bounded distance from the top has a certificate with tail below $B$, so
along any sequence of monic multiples with $b_{u+1}$ tending to the bound all
$u$ exempt coefficients must move away from the top, as the construction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:partial} moves them to the bottom.  When \eqref{eq:partial} fails, Section~\ref{sec:value} computes the
infimum.

\section{The infimum when the criterion fails}\label{sec:value}

Keep the notation of Theorem~\ref{thm:converse}: $r_1<\cdots<r_L$ are
distinct, $y_i=1/r_i$, and $m=L-u$.  For finite $X\subset\N_{>0}$ and
$0\le k<L$ put
\[
  \tau_k(X)=\inf\bigl\{\Tail(v):v\text{ a certificate for }X\text{ on }
  y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L\bigr\},
  \quad \tau(X)=\tau_0(X),\quad \tau^*=\tau_u(\varnothing).
\]
A certificate on $c$ nodes is \emph{full} if it has $c-1$ prescribed zeros.
It is then unique, and by the zero bound it has no other zeros and keeps one
sign past the largest.  By \eqref{eq:weak} every monic multiple $F$ has
$b_{u+1}(F)\ge1/\tau(X_F)$, where $X_F$ holds the distances from the top of
its $u$ largest nonleading coefficients.  So the infimum is set by the worst
placement.  The first result says that it is exactly that, and that exempt
coefficients escaping to the bottom cost $\tau^*$, the best tail on the
surviving roots alone.

\begin{theorem}[The infimum as a placement game]\label{thm:value}
Let $1<r_1<\cdots<r_L$ and $0\le u\le L-1$.  Then
\[
  \inf_Qb_{u+1}\Bigl(Q\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)\Bigr)=\frac1T,
  \qquad T=\sup_{|X|=u}\tau(X)\ge\tau^*,
\]
over monic $Q\in\R[x]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The lower bound $b_{u+1}(F)\ge1/\tau(X_F)\ge1/T$ is \eqref{eq:weak}.

For the upper bound fix $X$ with $|X|=u$ and $D\ge\max(L,\max X)$, and let
$\tau_D(X)$ be the least $\sum_{d=1}^D|v_d|$ over certificates $v$ for $X$.
It is positive, since a certificate vanishing at $1,\ldots,D$ would vanish
identically.  Minimizing the largest $|f_j|$ over the positions $j<D$ with
$D-j\notin X$, subject to $F=x^D+\sum_{j<D}f_jx^j$ vanishing at every $r_i$,
is a feasible linear program.  Write $\mu_i$ for the multiplier of
$F(r_i)=0$, and put $\lambda_i=-\mu_ir_i^D$ and $v_d=\sum_i\lambda_iy_i^d$.
The coefficient of $f_j$ in the Lagrangian is then $-v_{D-j}$, and the dual
program is to maximize $v_0$ subject to $v_d=0$ for $d\in X$ and
$\sum_{d\le D,\,d\notin X}|v_d|\le1$.  Its value is $1/\tau_D(X)$.  By
linear-programming duality some monic multiple of degree $D$ has every
coefficient off the $u$ positions of $X$ at most $1/\tau_D(X)$ in magnitude,
so its $b_{u+1}$ is at most $1/\tau_D(X)$.

Two limits finish the proof.  First, $\tau_D(X)$ is nondecreasing in $D$ and
at most $\tau(X)$.  Take minimizers $v^{(D)}=\sum_i\lambda^{(D)}_iy_i^d$.
Their values at $L$ fixed points of $\{0,1,\ldots\}\setminus X$, one of them
$0$, are bounded by $\max(1,\tau(X))$.  The matrix $(y_i^d)$ at $L$ distinct
points is nonsingular by the zero bound, so the $\lambda^{(D)}$ are bounded.
A subsequence converges to a certificate $v$ for $X$ with
$\sum_{d\le N}|v_d|\le\lim_D\tau_D(X)$ for every $N$, whence
$\tau(X)\le\Tail(v)\le\lim_D\tau_D(X)$.  So the infimum is at most $1/\tau(X)$
for every $X$, that is, at most $1/T$.

Second, put $E_\nu=\{\nu+1,\ldots,\nu+u\}$ and suppose
$\tau(E_\nu)\le\tau^*-\varepsilon$ along a subsequence.  Take certificates
$v^\nu=\sum_i\lambda^\nu_iy_i^d$ for $E_\nu$ with
$\Tail(v^\nu)\le\tau^*-\varepsilon/2$.  For $\nu\ge L$ their values at
$0,\ldots,L-1$ are bounded, so we may assume $\lambda^\nu\to\lambda$.  The $u$
equations $v^\nu_e=0$, $e\in E_\nu$, read
$A_\nu\lambda^\nu_{\le u}=-R_\nu\lambda^\nu_{>u}$ with
$A_\nu=(y_j^e)_{e\in E_\nu,\,j\le u}$ and
$R_\nu=(y_j^e)_{e\in E_\nu,\,j>u}$.  By Cramer's rule each entry of
$A_\nu^{-1}R_\nu$ is, up to sign, a ratio $\alpha_J/\alpha_{J^*}$ of the
minors in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:far}, with $k=u$ and $J\ne J^*$.  So it
is at most $\eta_\nu\to0$, and $\lambda_{\le u}=0$.  Then
$v=\sum_{i>u}\lambda_iy_i^d$ is a certificate on the surviving nodes, and as
before $\Tail(v)\le\tau^*-\varepsilon/2$, against the definition of $\tau^*$.
Hence $\liminf_\nu\tau(E_\nu)\ge\tau^*$, and $T\ge\tau^*$.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:far} supplies the reverse inequality, so $\tau(E_\nu)\to\tau^*$:
the escaping placement costs exactly $\tau^*$.  There is also a dual side.  If
$v=\sum_{i>u}\lambda_iy_i^d$ is a certificate on the surviving nodes and $w$
is a bounded sequence with $w_0=1$ whose series $W(y)=\sum_dw_dy^d$ vanishes
at $y_{u+1},\ldots,y_L$, then $0=\sum_i\lambda_iW(y_i)=\sum_dw_dv_d$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dualpair}
  1\le\sup_{d\ge1}|w_d|\cdot\Tail(v).
\end{equation}
Such $W$ are the $\widetilde P_uH$ with $H(0)=1$.  Taking
$H=\widetilde M/(1-y)$ for a monic $M\in\R[x]$ with reverse $\widetilde M$
makes $w_d$ the partial sums $S_d(P_uM)$, as in \eqref{eq:wgen}.  So
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiplier}
  \inf_Qb_{u+1}\Bigl(Q\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)\Bigr)\le\frac1{\tau^*}
  \le\max_{j\ge1}\bigl|S_j(P_uM)\bigr|
  \qquad\text{for every monic }M\in\R[x].
\end{equation}
At $M=1$ this is the construction of Proposition~\ref{prop:partial}.  A
witness $W$ with $\sup_{d\ge1}|w_d|=1/\Tail(v)$ proves that $v$ attains
$\tau^*$.

Whether $T=\tau^*$, so that the escaping placement is extremal, comes down to
covering the finite placements.  The deletion step of Theorem~2.2 lifts a
certificate on fewer nodes to one on all of them.

\begin{theorem}[Lifting]\label{thm:lift}
Let $2\le r_1<\cdots<r_L$ and $0\le k\le u$, and let $v$ be a full
certificate on $y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$ with zero set $Z$ and $g=\max Z$ ($g=0$
if $Z$ is empty).  Then $\tau(X)\le\Tail(v)$ for every $X$ of size $u$ with
$X\subseteq Z\cup(g,\infty)$ and $|X\setminus Z|\le k$.  The inequality is
strict if $k\ge1$ and the padded set $Z^+$ below is not $\{1,\ldots,L-1\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Add to $Z\cup X$ integers above $\max(Z\cup X)$ until it has $L-1$ elements,
and call the result $Z^+$.  It exceeds $Z$ by $k$ elements, all above $g$.
The full certificate $v^+$ on $y_1,\ldots,y_L$ with zero set $Z^+$ is a
certificate for $X$.  Delete the largest zero of $Z^+$ together with the
largest node $y_1$.  The nodes are at most $1/2$, so the deletion step in the
proof of Theorem~2.2 bounds $\Tail(v^+)$ by the tail of the full certificate
on $y_2,\ldots,y_L$ with the remaining zeros, strictly unless
$Z^+=\{1,\ldots,L-1\}$.  In that case Lemma~2.1 gives the two tails as
products that differ by the factor $1/(r_1-1)\le1$.  Repeat $k$ times.  Each
removed zero is the largest remaining, because the added ones all exceed $g$,
and the last step lands on $v$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One exempt coefficient]\label{cor:uone}
Let $2\le r_1<\cdots<r_L$ and $u=1$, and suppose $\tau^*$ is attained by a
full certificate on $y_2,\ldots,y_L$ with zero set $Z^*$ and $g=\max Z^*$.
Then
\[
  \inf_Qb_2\Bigl(Q\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)\Bigr)
  =\frac1{\max\bigl(\tau^*,\ \max_{w\in G}\tau(\{w\})\bigr)},
  \qquad G=\{1,\ldots,g-1\}\setminus Z^*.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Here $T=\sup_w\tau(\{w\})$.  If $w\notin G$, then $w\in Z^*$ or $w>g$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:lift} with $k=1$ gives $\tau(\{w\})\le\tau^*$.  Since
$T\ge\tau^*$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:value}, the maximum is $T$.
\end{proof}

For $u\ge2$ the same covering is a finite search.  Start from
$(A,k)=(\varnothing,u)$.  Choose a full certificate on $y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$
whose zero set $Z$ contains $A$ and whose tail is at most $\tau^*$.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:lift} it covers every $X\supseteq A$ whose other $k$
elements lie in $Z\cup(\max Z,\infty)$.  Recurse on $(A\cup\{w\},k-1)$ for
each $w<\max Z$ outside $Z\cup A$.  If the search closes, $T=\tau^*$ and the
infimum is $1/\tau^*$.

The search collapses to a single step.  One node is added and one zero is
deleted at a time, and the zero deleted must be the largest.

\begin{proposition}[Step reduction]\label{prop:step}
Let $1<r_1<\cdots<r_L$ and $0\le u\le L-1$.  Suppose that for every
$0\le k<u$ and every set $X$ of $u-k$ positive integers
\begin{equation}\label{eq:step}
  \tau_k(X)\le\tau_{k+1}\bigl(X\setminus\{\max X\}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Then $T=\tau^*$, and the infimum of $b_{u+1}$ is $1/\tau^*$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Chaining \eqref{eq:step} from $k=0$ upwards, each step deleting the largest
remaining element, gives
$\tau(X)=\tau_0(X)\le\tau_u(\varnothing)=\tau^*$ for every $X$ of size $u$, so
$T\le\tau^*$; Theorem~\ref{thm:value} gives the reverse.
\end{proof}

Deleting $\min X$ instead is false: at $(2,3,4,5)$ with $u=2$ and
$X=\{2,3\}$, $\tau_0(\{2,3\})=\tfrac1{24}$ against
$\tau_1(\{3\})=\tfrac{35}{1128}$, a ratio of $1.34$, while
$\tau_1(\{2\})=\tfrac1{24}$ meets it.  Theorem~\ref{thm:lift} with $k=1$
proves \eqref{eq:step} whenever the placement and the certificate it is lifted
from are aligned.

\begin{proposition}[The step in the aligned cases]\label{prop:stepeasy}
Let $2\le r_1<\cdots<r_L$, let $X$ be finite and nonempty with $g=\max X$ and
$X'=X\setminus\{g\}$, and let $v^-$ be a full certificate on
$y_{k+2},\ldots,y_L$ whose zero set $Z^-$ contains $X'$.  If $g\in Z^-$ or
$g>\max Z^-$, then some certificate for $X$ on $y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$ has tail at
most $\Tail(v^-)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $Z^+=Z^-\cup\{g\}$ in the second case, and $Z^+=Z^-\cup\{N\}$ with any
$N>\max Z^-$ in the first.  Either way $Z^+\supseteq X$ has $L-k-1$ elements
and its largest exceeds all the others, so the full certificate $v^+$ on
$y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$ with zero set $Z^+$ is a certificate for $X$; deleting
that largest zero together with the largest node $y_{k+1}$ is the deletion step
in the proof of Theorem~2.2, which bounds $\Tail(v^+)$ by $\Tail(v^-)$ because
the nodes are at most $1/2$.
\end{proof}

Where a zero is displaced to is not a search.  Adding one node to a certificate
and one zero to its zero set moves it along a line, on which the tail is a
weighted sum of distances.

\begin{lemma}[Sign rule for the pencil]\label{lem:pencil}
Let $n=L-k-1$, let $A$ be a set of $n-1$ positive integers, let $F$ be the full
certificate on $y_{k+2},\ldots,y_L$ with zero set $A$, and let $H\ne0$ be an
exponential sum on $y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$ with $H_0=0$ and $H_a=0$ for $a\in A$.
Then $H$ is unique up to scale, the certificates on $y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$
vanishing on $A$ are exactly $F+tH$ with $t\in\mathbb R$, and for every $d\ge1$
with $d\notin A$
\[
  \sgn F_d=(-1)^{|\{a\in A\,:\,a<d\}|},\qquad
  \sgn H_d=\varepsilon\,(-1)^{1+|\{a\in A\,:\,a<d\}|}
\]
for one fixed $\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}$.  In particular $F_dH_d$ has a single
sign throughout.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$H$ solves $n$ homogeneous conditions in $n+1$ unknowns, a system nonsingular
by the zero bound, so it is unique up to scale; adding $tH$ to $F$ preserves
$v_0=1$ and the zeros on $A$, and the dimensions match.  Now $F$ is an
exponential sum on $n$ nodes with the $n-1$ zeros $A$, so by the zero bound it
has no others and changes sign at each of them, and $F_0=1>0$.  Likewise $H$ is
one on $n+1$ nodes with the $n$ zeros $\{0\}\cup A$ and no others; for $d\ge1$
the count of zeros below $d$ includes the one at $0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The displaced zero is a weighted median]\label{prop:median}
In the notation of Lemma~\ref{lem:pencil}, let $N\ge1$ with $N\notin A$.  The
full certificate on $y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$ with zero set $A\cup\{N\}$ is
$F+t_NH$ with $t_N=-F_N/H_N$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:median}
  \Tail\bigl(A\cup\{N\}\bigr)
  =\sum_{d\ge1}\Bigl|\,|F_d|-\theta_N|H_d|\,\Bigr|
  =\sum_{d\ge1}|H_d|\,\Bigl|\theta_N-\frac{|F_d|}{|H_d|}\Bigr|,
  \qquad\theta_N=\frac{|F_N|}{|H_N|}.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is convex and piecewise linear in $\theta$ with breakpoints
exactly the $\theta_N$, so
\[
  \min_{N\ge1,\;N\notin A}\Tail\bigl(A\cup\{N\}\bigr)
  =\min_{\theta\ge0}\sum_{d\ge1}|H_d|\,\Bigl|\theta-\frac{|F_d|}{|H_d|}\Bigr|,
\]
attained at the weighted median of $\bigl(|F_d|/|H_d|\bigr)_{d\ge1,\,d\notin A}$
with weights $|H_d|$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:pencil} the certificates vanishing on $A$ form the line
$F+tH$, and $t_N$ is the unique parameter killing $N$.  There $t_NH$ opposes
$F$, so by the sign rule it opposes $F$ at every $d\ge1$, whence
$|F_d+t_NH_d|=\bigl||F_d|-|t_N|\,|H_d|\bigr|$ with $|t_N|=\theta_N$, which is
\eqref{eq:median}.  Each summand is $|H_d|$ times the modulus of an affine
function of $\theta$ vanishing at $|F_d|/|H_d|$, so the sum is convex and
piecewise linear with those breakpoints and attains its minimum at one of them;
a weighted sum of distances is minimized at the weighted median.
\end{proof}

The median is an order statistic, because the points it ranges over are
already sorted by $d$.

\begin{lemma}[The ratio is monotone]\label{lem:ratio}
In the notation of Lemma~\ref{lem:pencil}, $d\mapsto|F_d|/|H_d|$ is strictly
decreasing on $\{d\ge0:d\notin A\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $c\in\mathbb R$ the sequence $H-cF$ is an exponential sum on the $n+1$
nodes $y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$, and is not identically zero, since $F$ gives weight
$0$ to $y_{k+1}$ and $H$ does not.  By the zero bound it has at most $n$ real
zeros with multiplicity, and it vanishes on $A$, which is $n-1$ of them.  So
$\Phi=H/F$ takes each value at most once off $A$, and takes none of them at an
$a\in A$ as well, as that would be a second zero there.  On each component of
$[0,\infty)\setminus A$ the ratio $\Phi$ is continuous, and $F$ and $H$ have a
simple zero at each $a\in A$, so $\Phi$ extends continuously across it.  Being
continuous and injective on $[0,\infty)$, $\Phi$ is strictly monotone; and
$\Phi(0)=0$ because $H_0=0$, while $|\Phi(d)|\to\infty$ because $|H_d|$ decays
like $y_{k+1}^d$ and $|F_d|$ like $y_{k+2}^d$, with $y_{k+2}<y_{k+1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The displaced zero is a half-mass point]\label{cor:halfmass}
The minimum in Proposition~\ref{prop:median} is attained at
\[
  N=\max\Bigl\{d\ge1,\ d\notin A\ :\
  \sum_{e\ge d}|H_e|\ \ge\ \tfrac12\sum_{e\ge1}|H_e|\Bigr\}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:ratio} the order of the points $|F_d|/|H_d|$ is the reverse
of the order of $d$, so the weights $|H_d|$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:median}
accumulate from the largest $d$ downwards.
\end{proof}

The displacement therefore needs no window, and $N$ need not exceed $\max A$.
Taking $\theta=0$ in \eqref{eq:median} returns $\Tail(F)$, and the breakpoints
are positive and tend to $0$, since $|F_d|/|H_d|$ decays like
$(y_{k+2}/y_{k+1})^d$; so the convex minimum is at most the value at $0$.

\begin{corollary}[A displaced zero always pays for a node]\label{cor:nocut}
With $F$ and $H$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:pencil} and nodes anywhere in $(0,1)$,
some $N$ gives $\Tail(A\cup\{N\})\le\Tail(F)$.
\end{corollary}

The node cutoff is not needed for this.  For a \emph{fixed} zero set the
deletion step reverses beyond the cutoff, as on $(1/3,1/2,9/10)$ above; once
the displaced zero is chosen by Proposition~\ref{prop:median} it cannot.

So \eqref{eq:step} can fail only through a misalignment: the minimizing $Z^-$
has $g<\max Z^-$ and $g\notin Z^-$, and no zero can be added to $Z^-$ above its
largest to reach $g$.  Freeing the largest zero does not repair it, because
replacing $\max Z^-$ by $g$ throws away the displacement $Z^-$ earned and
leaves a consecutive set, where Theorem~2.2 is an equality: at
$(\tfrac{503}{250},\tfrac{2053}{1000},\tfrac{527}{250},\tfrac{11}5,
\tfrac{1177}{500})$ with $u=1$ and $X=\{2\}$ that turns $Z^-=\{1,3,6\}$ into
$\{1,2,3\}$, and no displaced lift of it comes within a factor $1.40$.
Sliding down the \emph{smallest} zero above $g$ keeps the displacement.  Nor
can the slide be dispensed with: adjoining $g$ to $Z^-$ and displacing nothing
gives a zero set of the right size, but its tail exceeds $\Tail(v^-)$ in $245$
of the $379$ straddling instances we put to it, at
$(\tfrac{2013}{1000},\tfrac{257}{125},\tfrac{2127}{1000},\tfrac{1071}{500},
\tfrac{549}{250})$ with $u=1$ and $X=\{2\}$ by a factor $2.01$.

Both the slide and the displacement are choices of a point on one line, and
that line carries its own minimum.  Fix a set $B$ of $L-k-3$ positive
integers and let
\[
  \mathcal P=\bigl\{v \text{ on } y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L\ :\ v_0=1,\
  v_b=0\ (b\in B)\bigr\},
\]
a two-parameter family on which $\Tail$ is convex.  For $a\notin B$ write
$\ell_a=\{v\in\mathcal P:v_a=0\}$, and $\ell_\infty$ for the certificates in
$\mathcal P$ giving weight $0$ to $y_{k+1}$, that is, the full certificates on
$y_{k+2},\ldots,y_L$ whose zero set contains $B$.  Each $\ell_a$ is a pencil as
in Lemma~\ref{lem:pencil}, so $\Tail$ restricted to it is the convex function
of Proposition~\ref{prop:median}, minimized at the half-mass point of
Corollary~\ref{cor:halfmass}; two of these lines meet in the full certificate
with zero set $B\cup\{a,b\}$, which we write $p_{ab}$, and $\ell_a$ meets
$\ell_\infty$ in the full certificate $F_a$ on $y_{k+2},\ldots,y_L$ with zero
set $B\cup\{a\}$.  The slide-down rule is then one inequality between a line
and a point.

\begin{proposition}[Slide reduction]\label{prop:slide}
Let $2\le r_1<\cdots<r_L$, let $X$ be finite with $g=\max X$ and
$X'=X\setminus\{g\}$, and let $Z^-$ attain $\tau_{k+1}(X')$ with $g\notin Z^-$
and $g<\max Z^-$.  Put $M=\min\{z\in Z^-:z>g\}$ and $B=Z^-\setminus\{M\}$.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slide}
  \min\{\Tail(v):v\in\ell_g\}\ \le\ \Tail(F_M),
\end{equation}
then \eqref{eq:step} holds at $X$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$B$ has $L-k-3$ elements, $g$ and $M$ lie outside it and no element of $B$
lies between them.  Every $v\in\ell_g$ vanishes on $B\cup\{g\}\supseteq X$,
because $X'\subseteq Z^-$ and $M>g=\max X$ puts $M$ outside $X$; so the left
side of \eqref{eq:slide} is at least $\tau_k(X)$, while
$\Tail(F_M)=\Tail(Z^-)=\tau_{k+1}(X')$.
\end{proof}

Together with Proposition~\ref{prop:stepeasy} this covers every case of
\eqref{eq:step}, and two alignments of the minimizers settle
\eqref{eq:slide} by convexity alone.

\begin{proposition}[Two aligned cases of the slide]\label{prop:slidealigned}
Let $B$, $g<M$ be as in \eqref{eq:slide}, let $M^*$ minimize $\Tail(F_a)$
over $a\notin B$, and let $N'$ be the half-mass point of
Corollary~\ref{cor:halfmass} for $A=B\cup\{M\}$ on $y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $M^*\le g$, then $\Tail(F_g)\le\Tail(F_M)$, and \eqref{eq:slide}
  holds by Corollary~\ref{cor:nocut}.
\item If $N'\le g$, then $\Tail(p_{gM})\le\Tail(F_M)$: adjoining $g$ to $Z^-$
  costs nothing, and \eqref{eq:slide} holds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On $\ell_\infty$ the points $F_a$ are ordered by $a$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:ratio}
applied on $y_{k+2},\ldots,y_L$), so in the first case $F_g$ lies between
$F_{M^*}$ and $F_M$, and convexity of $\Tail$ along the line gives
$\Tail(F_g)\le\max(\Tail(F_{M^*}),\Tail(F_M))=\Tail(F_M)$.  On $\ell_M$ the
points $p_{Ma}$ are ordered by $a$ with $F_M$ at the end, so in the second case
$p_{gM}$ lies between $F_M$ and the minimizer $p_{MN'}$, and convexity gives
$\Tail(p_{gM})\le\Tail(F_M)$.
\end{proof}

The remaining case, $M^*>g$ and $N'>g$, is where the slide must displace.
There \eqref{eq:slide} is false in general.

\begin{proposition}[The slide fails for a general zero set]\label{prop:slidefails}
Let $L=9$, $k=0$, $y_i=\tfrac12-(i-1)\cdot10^{-4}$, $B=\{1,2,3,11,12,13\}$,
$g=4$ and $M=6$.  Then $M=M^*$, and every $v\in\ell_g$ has
$\Tail(v)\ge1.026\,\Tail(F_M)$.  In the confluent limit of
Proposition~\ref{prop:confluent} with all nine nodes at $\tfrac12$, where the
full certificate with zero set $Z$ is $d\mapsto2^{-d}\prod_{z\in Z}(1-d/z)$,
the same $B$, $g$, $M$ give
\[
  \min_{N}\Tail\bigl(B\cup\{4,N\}\bigr)
  =\Tail\bigl(\{1,2,3,4,10,11,12,13\}\bigr)=\tfrac3{143}
  =\tfrac{36}{35}\Tail\bigl(B\cup\{6\}\bigr),
\]
where $\Tail(B\cup\{6\})=\tfrac{35}{1716}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exact rational arithmetic.  The minimum over $\ell_g$ is at its half-mass
point $N=10$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:halfmass}, and the tails at $N\le40$ were
also compared directly; in the confluent case every $N\le399$ was checked and
the limit $N\to\infty$ is $\Tail(B\cup\{4\})=2.01\,\Tail(B\cup\{6\})$.
\end{proof}

So the case analysis does not disappear.  The hypothesis
Proposition~\ref{prop:slide} actually supplies, that $B\cup\{M\}$ is a
minimizer of $\tau_{k+1}$, is stronger than the hypothesis of
\eqref{eq:slide}, and at the root set of Proposition~\ref{prop:slidefails} it
is not met: the minimizer for $X'=\{1,2,3\}$ on $y_2,\ldots,y_9$ is
$\{1,2,3,6,8,12,17\}$, and \eqref{eq:step} holds at $X=\{1,2,3,4\}$ with ratio
$0.70$.  We know no root set at which \eqref{eq:step} fails: none among
$47638$ instances over integer, rational and clustered root sets to $L=6$,
none among $1352$ further steps with $u\le3$, and none among the $9828$
placements of size $L-2$ inside $\{1,\ldots,15\}$ on the clustered nodes
$\tfrac12-(i-1)\cdot10^{-4}$ with $L\le8$.  Where the displaced zero goes is
not settled either: the half-mass point $N'$ of $\ell_M$ wins in most
straddling instances, but at the confluent limit with $B=\{1,\ldots,24\}$,
$g=25$ and $M=50$, where $M$ is the minimizer on $\ell_\infty$, the vertex
$p_{gN'}$ has $N'=55$ and tail $1.031\,\Tail(F_M)$ while $p_{g,51}$ meets
$\Tail(F_M)$ exactly and $p_{g,52}$ falls below it.  What does hold, at every $g$ and whatever lies above it, is the step from a
zero set whose part below $g$ is an initial segment, by a certificate that is
not a vertex at all: smoothing the minimizing certificate against the new
node.

\begin{theorem}[The step from an initial segment]\label{thm:slideinitial}
Let $y_1,\ldots,y_{c-1}\in(0,1)$ be distinct, let $y_c\in(0,\tfrac12]$ be a
further node, let $Z^-$ be a set of $c-2$ positive integers and $g\notin Z^-$
with $Z^-\cap(0,g)=\{1,\ldots,k\}$, and let $F$ be the full certificate on
$y_1,\ldots,y_{c-1}$ with zero set $Z^-$.  Then some certificate on all $c$
nodes vanishing on $\{1,\ldots,k\}\cup\{g\}$ has tail at most $\Tail(F)$, and
for $g=k+1$ at most $\tfrac{y_c}{1-y_c}\Tail(F)$.  If $Z^-$ has a single
element $M$ above $g$, the certificate can be taken full, with zero set
$\{1,\ldots,k\}\cup\{g,N\}$, so \eqref{eq:slide} holds for
$B=\{1,\ldots,k\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $F(x)=\sum_dF_dx^d=N(x)/\prod_{i<c}(1-y_ix)$ with $\deg N\le c-2$, and
put
\[
  v(x)=\frac{1-y_cxF(x)}{1-y_cx}
      =\frac{\prod_{i<c}(1-y_ix)-y_cxN(x)}{\prod_{i\le c}(1-y_ix)} .
\]
The numerator has degree at most $c-1$ and the poles are simple, so $v$ is an
exponential sum on the $c$ nodes; expanding, $v_0=1$ and
\[
  v_d=-\sum_{1\le j<d}F_j\,y_c^{\,d-j}\qquad(d\ge1).
\]
For $b\le k+1$ every $j<b$ lies in $Z^-$, so $v_b=0$.  Summing the geometric
factors,
$\Tail(v)\le\sum_{j\ge1}|F_j|\sum_{i\ge1}y_c^i
=\tfrac{y_c}{1-y_c}\Tail(F)\le\Tail(F)$, the one use of the cutoff.  This
settles $g=k+1$.  For $g>k+1$ the sum for $v_g$ runs over $k<j<g$; by the
zero bound $F$ has exactly the zeros $Z^-$, so it keeps one sign from $k$ to
the least element of $Z^-$ above $g$, or to infinity if there is none, and
$v_g$ has the sign opposite to $F_g$.  The segment from $F$ to $v$ consists of sums on the
$c$ nodes with $v_0=1$ vanishing on $\{1,\ldots,k\}$, and it crosses the
hyperplane $v_g=0$ at $w=(1-\lambda)F+\lambda v$ with
$\lambda=F_g/(F_g-v_g)\in(0,1)$; $\Tail$ is convex, so
$\Tail(w)\le\max(\Tail(F),\Tail(v))=\Tail(F)$.  When $Z^-=\{1,\ldots,k\}\cup\{M\}$
the whole segment lies in $\mathcal P$ for $B=\{1,\ldots,k\}$, so $w\in\ell_g$,
and by Proposition~\ref{prop:median} the minimum over $\ell_g$ is at a vertex
$p_{gN}$, a full certificate with the claimed tail.
\end{proof}

Since $\tau_k(X)$ is an infimum over all certificates for $X$, the theorem
proves \eqref{eq:step} at every $X$ whose minimizer for $X'$ has an initial
segment below $\max X$, whatever its zeros above.  It does not need
minimality: at the root set of Proposition~\ref{prop:slidefails}, where the
slide from $\{1,2,3,6,11,12,13\}$ to $g=4$ fails on every vertex, the smoothed
certificate vanishes on $\{1,2,3,4\}$ with tail $0.82$ times the tail of that
zero set, and for $g=5$ the crossing point has tail $0.48$ times it.  Only the
new node is held to the cutoff, and the others may exceed it; with
$y_c>\tfrac12$ the conclusion fails in $59$ of $114$ instances tried, so the
hypothesis is the right one.  The factor $\tfrac{y_c}{1-y_c}$ is sharp: at the
confluent limit with all nodes at $\tfrac12$, $g=k+1$ and $M=2k+2$ the ratio in
\eqref{eq:slide} is $(2k+3)/(2k+4)$ for $k\le8$, exactly, where
$\Tail(B\cup\{M\})=\binom{2k+2}{k+1}4^{-k-1}$.  The construction does not
reach a gap below $g$, where
$v_b=-\sum_{j<b,\,j\notin Z^-}F_jy_c^{b-j}$ need not vanish at the first
element of $Z^-$ above the gap.

What remains is one inequality with the right hypothesis, and the hypothesis
is the part of minimality that the counterexample lacks.  Write
$Z^-=B_0\cup A$ with $B_0=Z^-\cap(0,g)$ and $A=Z^-\cap(g,\infty)$, and
$A+1=\{a+1:a\in A\}$.  The rule is not to displace one zero to a half-mass
point but to shift every zero above $g$ up by one:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shift}
  \Tail\bigl(B_0\cup\{g\}\cup(A+1)\bigr)\le\Tail(B_0\cup A)
  \quad\text{whenever}\quad
  \Tail(B_0\cup A)\le\Tail\bigl(B_0\cup A\setminus\{a\}\cup\{a+1\}\bigr)
\end{equation}
for every $a\in A$ with $a+1\notin A$, the left side on
$y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$ and the others on $y_{k+2},\ldots,y_L$.
For $|A|=1$ it is proved below (Corollary~\ref{cor:shiftone}).  For
$|A|\ge2$ we have no proof, and no counterexample: it holds in all
$7023$ instances satisfying its hypothesis among $8523$ tried with $L\le9$,
over the clustered nodes $\tfrac12-(i-1)\cdot10^{-4}$ with $g\le7$ and $A$
within $6$ of $g$, and over $300$ random node sets at $L=7$, while $283$
instances without the hypothesis violate the conclusion.  The largest
ratio seen is $0.9993$, at the clustered nodes with $B_0=\{1,2,3,4\}$,
$g=5$, $A=\{10\}$, against $1$ in the confluent limit.

\begin{proposition}[The shift]\label{prop:shift}
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $Z^-$ attains $\tau_{k+1}(X')$ with $g\notin Z^-$ and $g<\max Z^-$,
  then the hypothesis of \eqref{eq:shift} holds at $Z^-$, and
  \eqref{eq:shift} gives \eqref{eq:step} at $X$.
\item For $A=\{M\}$ the hypothesis is $\Tail(F_M)\le\Tail(F_{M+1})$, that is,
  at least half the mass $\sum_{d\notin B_0}|H_d|$ of the direction $H$ of
  $\ell_\infty$ lies at $d\le M$; the conclusion is
  $\Tail(p_{g,M+1})\le\Tail(F_M)$.
\item In the confluent limit with all nodes at $\tfrac12$, $B_0=\{1,\ldots,k\}$,
  $g=k+1$ and $A=\{M\}$, the hypothesis and the conclusion of
  \eqref{eq:shift} are both equivalent to $M\ge2k+2$, and the conclusion is an
  equality exactly at $M=2k+2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) Every $a\in A$ exceeds $g=\max X$, so $a\notin X'$ and
$Z^-\setminus\{a\}\cup\{a+1\}$ is a zero set of the right size containing
$X'$ when $a+1\notin Z^-$; minimality of $Z^-$ is the hypothesis.  The left
side of \eqref{eq:shift} is the tail of a full certificate vanishing on
$B_0\cup\{g\}\supseteq X$, so it is at least $\tau_k(X)$, and the right side
is $\tau_{k+1}(X')$.

(2) Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:pencil} and Proposition~\ref{prop:median} on
$y_{k+2},\ldots,y_L$ with $A=B_0$, so that $H$ is the sum on those nodes
vanishing on $\{0\}\cup B_0$: $F_M$ and $F_{M+1}$ are the points
$\theta_M>\theta_{M+1}$ of \eqref{eq:median}, between which the breakpoints
below $\theta$ are exactly those with $d>M$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:ratio}; so
$\Tail(F_{M+1})-\Tail(F_M)
=(\theta_M-\theta_{M+1})\bigl(\sum_{d\le M}|H_d|-\sum_{d>M}|H_d|\bigr)$.

(3) Let $D$ have $\Pr(D=d)=\binom{d-1}{k}2^{-d}$ for $d\ge k+1$, the number of
fair coin tosses to the $(k+1)$st head, with mean $2k+2$, and let $G$ be an
independent geometric variable, $\Pr(G=j)=2^{-j}$ for $j\ge1$, so that $D+G$
is the number of tosses to the $(k+2)$nd head.  The certificate with zero set
$\{1,\ldots,k\}\cup\{M\}$ is $d\mapsto(-1)^k\Pr(D=d)(1-d/M)$, and since
$(d-k-1)\binom{d-1}{k}=(k+1)\binom{d-1}{k+1}$, the one with zero set
$\{1,\ldots,k+1\}\cup\{N\}$ is $d\mapsto(-1)^{k+1}\Pr(D+G=d)(1-d/N)$; hence
$\Tail(\{1,\ldots,k\}\cup\{M\})=\E|D-M|/M$ and
$\Tail(\{1,\ldots,k+1\}\cup\{N\})=\E|D+G-N|/N$.  For integers $x$ and $N$ a
direct sum gives $\E|x+G-N|=|x-N|-2+4\cdot2^{-(N-x)_+}$, and
$\E|D-m-1|-\E|D-m|=2\Pr(D\le m)-1$.  With $N=M+1$, conditioning on $D$ and
using $\sum_{d\le M}\binom{d-1}{k}=\binom{M}{k+1}$,
\[
  (M+1)\Tail\bigl(\{1,\ldots,k+1\}\cup\{M+1\}\bigr)
  =\E|D-M|+1-2\Pr(D\le M)+2\binom{M}{k+1}2^{-M},
\]
while $(M+1)\Tail(\{1,\ldots,k\}\cup\{M+1\})=\E|D-M|+2\Pr(D\le M)-1$.  Next,
$(2k+2-d)\Pr(D=d)=(k+1)2^{-d}\bigl[\binom{d-1}{k}-\binom{d-1}{k+1}\bigr]$ by
Pascal's rule, and summing over $d\le M$ telescopes against the two
head-count events to $(k+1)\binom{M}{k+1}2^{-M}$; with
$\E|D-M|=(2k+2-M)+2\sum_{d\le M}(M-d)\Pr(D=d)$ this gives
\[
  \E|D-M|=(M-2k-2)\bigl(2\Pr(D\le M)-1\bigr)+2(k+1)\binom{M}{k+1}2^{-M}.
\]
Substituting, the hypothesis $\E|D-M|\le M(2\Pr(D\le M)-1)$ and the
conclusion $M(1-2\Pr(D\le M))+2M\binom{M}{k+1}2^{-M}\le\E|D-M|$ both reduce
to $\binom{M}{k+1}2^{-M}\le2\Pr(D\le M)-1$.  In terms of the number $S$ of
heads in $M$ tosses, $\Pr(D\le M)=\Pr(S\ge k+1)$ and
$\binom{M}{k+1}2^{-M}=\Pr(S=k+1)$, so the condition is
$\Pr(S\ge k+2)\ge\Pr(S\le k)=\Pr(S\ge M-k)$, which holds exactly when
$M-k\ge k+2$, with equality exactly when $M=2k+2$.
\end{proof}

The confluent block is therefore the case where \eqref{eq:shift} is sharp, and
its hypothesis is exactly what the conclusion needs.  Away from it there is
room: at the same $B_0$ and $g$ with $M>2k+2$ the ratio falls below $1$, and
at $M=2k+2$ the vertex $p_{g,M+2}$, which is the smoothed certificate of
Theorem~\ref{thm:slideinitial} there, reaches $(2k+3)/(2k+4)$.  The plane
$\mathcal P$ makes the shape of the question visible.  With $H$ as in (2),
$K$ the sum on $y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$ vanishing on $\{0\}\cup B_0\cup\{M+1\}$,
$\theta_d=K_d/H_d$ and $\rho_d=-F_{M,d}/H_d$, every $v\in\mathcal P$ is
$v_d=H_d(\alpha+\beta\theta_d-\rho_d)$, so
$\Tail(v)=\sum_{d\notin B_0}|H_d|\,|\alpha+\beta\theta_d-\rho_d|$ is the
weighted $\ell^1$ error of an affine fit to $\rho$ against $\theta$; the
points $F_a$ are the constant fits, $p_{ab}$ the chords, and the zero bound
makes $\rho$ a concave function of $\theta$.  With the weights $|H_d|$
replaced by arbitrary positive weights the case $|A|=1$ of \eqref{eq:shift}
fails, so the exponential-sum structure of $H$ and the cutoff on $y_{k+1}$,
which enters only through $\theta$, are both essential.

The hypothesis can be separated from the geometry, and the geometry is a
theorem.  Write $F_M(d)$, $w(d)$ and so on for values at $d$, and
$E(d)$ for the value of an exponential sum $E$.

\begin{proposition}[The chord lemma]\label{prop:chord}
Let $B_0$ be a set of $L-k-3$ positive integers, $g>\max B_0$ and $M>g$.
Let $F_M$ and $F_{M+1}$ be the full certificates on $y_{k+2},\ldots,y_L$
with zero sets $B_0\cup\{M\}$ and $B_0\cup\{M+1\}$, and $w$ and $p$ the full
certificates on $y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$ with zero sets $B_0\cup\{g,M+1\}$ and
$B_0\cup\{M,M+1\}$.  Then $p=\lambda F_{M+1}+(1-\lambda)w$ for one
$\lambda\in(0,1)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chord}
  \Tail(F_M)\ \ge\ \lambda\,\Tail(F_{M+1})+(1-\lambda)\,\Tail(w),
\end{equation}
with equality if and only if $y_{k+1}=\tfrac12$ and $B_0=\{1,\ldots,g-1\}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Order.}  Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:pencil} and Proposition~\ref{prop:median}
with $A=B_0\cup\{M+1\}$ and $F=F_{M+1}$, and call the direction $K$: then
$p=F_{M+1}+t_MK$ and $w=F_{M+1}+t_gK$ with $t_N=-F_{M+1}(N)/K(N)$.  By the
sign rule $t_M$ and $t_g$ have one sign, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:ratio}
$|t_M|<|t_g|$, as $g<M$.  So $p=\lambda F_{M+1}+(1-\lambda)w$ with
$\lambda=1-t_M/t_g\in(0,1)$.

\emph{Signs.}  Put $\sigma=(-1)^{|B_0|}$ and $b=\max B_0$, or $b=0$ if
$B_0$ is empty.  Each of the four certificates is full, so by the zero bound
it has exactly its prescribed zeros, all simple, and it is positive at $0$.
Below $g$ all four have the sign $(-1)^{|B_0\cap(0,d)|}$ at $d$, and on the
intervals $(b,g)$, $(g,M)$, $(M,M+1)$, $(M+1,\infty)$ the signs are
$\sigma,\sigma,-\sigma,-\sigma$ for $F_M$; $\sigma,\sigma,\sigma,-\sigma$ for
$F_{M+1}$; $\sigma,-\sigma,-\sigma,\sigma$ for $w$; and
$\sigma,\sigma,-\sigma,\sigma$ for $p$.

\emph{Decomposition.}  Put $\delta_e=|F_M(e)|-|p(e)|$ and
$\gamma_d=\lambda|F_{M+1}(d)|+(1-\lambda)|w(d)|-|p(d)|$, so that the left
side of \eqref{eq:chord} minus the right is
$\sum_{e\ge1}\delta_e-\sum_{d\ge1}\gamma_d$; and let $G=F_M-p$ and
$E(d)=G(d-1)+2(1-\lambda)w(d)$, exponential sums on $y_{k+1},\ldots,y_L$.
For $d\le g$ the values $F_{M+1}(d)$ and $w(d)$ have one sign or vanish, so
$\gamma_d=0$.  For $g<d\le M$ they are opposed and $p(d)$ sides with
$F_{M+1}(d)$, so $|p(d)|=\lambda|F_{M+1}(d)|-(1-\lambda)|w(d)|$ and
$\gamma_d=2(1-\lambda)|w(d)|=-2(1-\lambda)\sigma w(d)$, while $F_M$ and $p$
have sign $\sigma$ or vanish on $[g,M]$, so $\delta_{d-1}=\sigma G(d-1)$.
Hence $\delta_{d-1}-\gamma_d=\sigma E(d)$ for $g<d\le M+1$, both sides being
$0$ at $d=M+1$.  For $d\ge M+2$ the values of $w$ and $p$ have sign $\sigma$
and those of $F_M$ and $F_{M+1}$ sign $-\sigma$, so
$\gamma_d=2\lambda|F_{M+1}(d)|=-2\lambda\sigma F_{M+1}(d)$ and
$\delta_e=-\sigma\bigl(F_M(e)+p(e)\bigr)$ for $e\ge M+1$; since $p$,
$F_{M+1}$ and $w$ vanish at $M+1$ and $p=\lambda F_{M+1}+(1-\lambda)w$,
\[
  \sum_{e\ge M+1}\delta_e-\sum_{d\ge M+2}\gamma_d
  =-\sigma\sum_{e\ge M+1}\bigl(F_M(e)-p(e)\bigr)
   -2(1-\lambda)\sigma\sum_{d\ge M+2}w(d)
  =-\sigma\sum_{d\ge M+2}E(d).
\]
Altogether
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chordsplit}
  \Tail(F_M)-\lambda\Tail(F_{M+1})-(1-\lambda)\Tail(w)
  =\sum_{e<g}\delta_e+\sigma\sum_{g<d\le M+1}E(d)-\sigma\sum_{d\ge M+2}E(d),
\end{equation}
and it remains to show that each of the three terms is nonnegative.

\emph{The first term.}  $G$ is an exponential sum on the $L-k$ nodes with
the $L-k-1$ zeros $\{0\}\cup B_0\cup\{M\}$, so by the zero bound it has no
others and changes sign at each.  Its weight on $y_{k+1}$ is minus that of
$p$, which has the sign $\sigma$ of $p$ at infinity, so $G$ has sign
$-\sigma$ on $(M,\infty)$, as does $F_M$; the two change sign together at
$B_0$ and at $M$, so they agree in sign everywhere.  On $(0,M)$ the values
of $F_M$ and $p$ also agree in sign, so $\delta_e=|G(e)|\ge0$ for $e<M$,
with $\delta_e=0$ exactly for $e\in B_0$.

\emph{Zeros of $E$.}  $E(M+1)=G(M)+2(1-\lambda)w(M+1)=0$.  Let
$S=\{0\}\cup B_0\cup\{g\}$; if $d-1\in S$ and $d\in S$ then
$G(d-1)=0=w(d)$ and $E(d)=0$.  Let $S$ consist of $m$ maximal runs of
consecutive integers $R_1,\ldots,R_m$, with $0\in R_1$ and $g\in R_m$; the
pairs give $|S|-m=L-k-1-m$ zeros, and with $M+1$ there are $L-k-m$ known
zeros.  At the start $q=r+1$ of each gap, where $r\in S$, $r<g$ and
$r+1\notin S$, we have $E(q)=2(1-\lambda)w(q)\ne0$, of sign
$(-1)^{|B_0\cap(0,q)|}$.  The weight of $F_M$ on $y_{k+1}$ is $0$, so the
weight of $E$ on $y_{k+1}$ is $(1-\lambda)(2-1/y_{k+1})$ times that of $w$,
which has the sign $\sigma$ of $w$ at infinity.  If $y_{k+1}<\tfrac12$ this
weight has sign $-\sigma$, $E$ has sign $-\sigma$ at infinity, and by the
zero bound $E$ has at most $L-k-1$ real zeros with multiplicity; if
$y_{k+1}=\tfrac12$ the weight is $0$, and $E$ is a sum on $L-k-1$ nodes with
at most $L-k-2$ zeros unless it vanishes identically.

\emph{Counting.}  Suppose first $m\ge2$, with gap starts
$q_1<\cdots<q_{m-1}$.  For $1\le j\le m-2$ the run $R_{j+1}\subseteq B_0$
lies in $(q_j,q_{j+1})$, supplies $|R_{j+1}|-1$ known zeros there, and the
signs of $E$ at $q_j$ and $q_{j+1}$ differ by the factor $(-1)^{|R_{j+1}|}$,
so $E$ has at least $|R_{j+1}|$ zeros in $(q_j,q_{j+1})$, counted with
multiplicity.  The run $R_1$ supplies $|R_1|-1$ zeros below $q_1$, and
beyond $q_{m-1}$ the known zeros are $a+1,\ldots,g$ with $a=\min R_m$, and
$M+1$: $|R_m|$ of them.  In all, at least $|S|-1=L-k-2$.  At $q_{m-1}$ the
sign of $E$ is $(-1)^{|B_0|-|R_m|+1}=\sigma(-1)^{|R_m|-1}$.  If
$y_{k+1}<\tfrac12$, the sign $-\sigma$ at infinity makes the number of zeros
beyond $q_{m-1}$ congruent to $|R_m|$ modulo $2$, and since the total is at
most $L-k-1$ that number is $|R_m|$ or $|R_m|+1$; so it is $|R_m|$.  If
$y_{k+1}=\tfrac12$ the total is at most $L-k-2$, so again exactly the known
zeros occur.  Either way the zeros of $E$ beyond $q_{m-1}$ are
$a+1,\ldots,g,M+1$, all simple, and from the sign at $q_{m-1}$, $E$ has sign
$\sigma$ on $(g,M+1)$ and $-\sigma$ on $(M+1,\infty)$.  If $m=1$, that is
$B_0=\{1,\ldots,g-1\}$, the known zeros $1,\ldots,g,M+1$ number $L-k-1$.
For $y_{k+1}<\tfrac12$ they are all the zeros, simple, and the sign $-\sigma$
at infinity gives $\sigma$ on $(g,M+1)$; for $y_{k+1}=\tfrac12$ the bound
$L-k-2$ forces $E\equiv0$.

So $\sigma E(d)\ge0$ for $g<d\le M+1$ and $\sigma E(d)\le0$ for $d\ge M+2$,
and \eqref{eq:chordsplit} proves \eqref{eq:chord}.  The first term of
\eqref{eq:chordsplit} vanishes exactly when $B_0=\{1,\ldots,g-1\}$; then
$E\equiv0$ if $y_{k+1}=\tfrac12$, and otherwise $\sigma E(g+1)>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The shift with one zero above $g$]\label{cor:shiftone}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:chord}, $\Tail(w)\le\Tail(F_M)$
whenever $\Tail(F_M)\le\Tail(F_{M+1})$.  So \eqref{eq:shift} holds for
$|A|=1$.  Consequently \eqref{eq:step} holds at each $X$ such that
$X\setminus\{\max X\}$ has a minimizing zero set with at most one element
above $\max X$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:chord} and the hypothesis,
$\Tail(F_M)\ge\lambda\Tail(F_M)+(1-\lambda)\Tail(w)$, and $\lambda<1$.  With
$A=\{M\}$ this is \eqref{eq:shift}, by part (2) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:shift}; part (1) turns it into \eqref{eq:step}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:stepeasy} covers a minimizer with no element above
$\max X$.
\end{proof}

The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:chord} counts integer slots: the zeros
of $E$ pinned at consecutive pairs of $\{0\}\cup B_0\cup\{g\}$, and its
signs pinned at the gap starts, are what fix its sign on $(g,M+1)$.  With
the elements of $B_0$ allowed to be real the count is unavailable, and
\eqref{eq:chord} is in fact false there, already in the confluent limit at
$B_0=\{1,\tfrac52\}$, $g=3$, $M=6$.  The cutoff enters once, through the
sign of the weight of $E$ on $y_{k+1}$, and for $y_{k+1}>\tfrac12$ that sign
flips.  In the block case $B_0=\{1,\ldots,g-1\}$ with $y_{k+1}=\tfrac12$ the
sum $E$ vanishes identically, and \eqref{eq:chord} is the identity behind
part (3) of Proposition~\ref{prop:shift}, the closed form of $\E|D-M|$.
What remains of \eqref{eq:shift} is $|A|\ge2$.  The chord does not
extend: write $M$ for the end of the first run of $A$, $C=B_0\cup A\setminus\{M\}$
for the common zeros, $F_1$ for the sum with zeros $C\cup\{M+1\}$ on the
small nodes and $w$ for the one with zeros $C\cup\{g,M+1\}$ on all $c$
nodes.  The identity \eqref{eq:chordsplit} holds verbatim with $B_0$
replaced by $C$, its first term is still nonnegative, but $E$ is now short
one forced zero for every run of $A$ above $M+1$, so its sign beyond $M+1$
is not pinned, and $\Tail(B_0\cup A)\ge\lambda\Tail(F_1)+(1-\lambda)\Tail(w)$
fails: at the clustered nodes with $L=9$, $B_0=\{1,2,3,5,6\}$, $g=7$,
$A=\{8,12\}$ the right side exceeds the left by $19\%$ of it, the whole
excess sitting in the far term.  No fixed pair of weights works in its
place: the feasible weight on $F_1$ is at most $0.683$ at $L\le7$ and at
least $0.794$ at $L=9$.  What does hold, in every instance tried, is the
sharp hypothesis-free form $\Tail(B_0\cup A)\ge\min(\Tail(F_1),\Tail(w))$,
and every chord failure has $\Tail(B_0\cup A)>\Tail(F_1)$.  A proof
therefore has to split cases: either the far sign of $E$ is pinned and
the chord argument goes through, or the unpinned run supplies a sign
change of $E$ beyond $M+1$ that must be shown to force
$\Tail(B_0\cup A)>\Tail(F_1)$.
What the median ranges over is not a one-parameter shape: the minimizing zero
set itself is not a consecutive block with one displaced zero.  At
$(2,3,4,5,6)$ with $u=0$ it is $\{1,2,4,6\}$, of tail $\tfrac{41}{12600}$,
against $0.003847$ for the best block $\{1,2,3\}$ with one displaced zero.

Whether it always closes is a question about one end of a multiple.  Fix the
placement.  Dividing the exempt roots out of a multiple controls every
coefficient below the exempt positions, by the geometric sum that proves
\eqref{eq:order}.

\begin{lemma}[Deflation]\label{lem:deflate}
Let $2\le r_1<\cdots<r_L$, let $F=\sum_jf_jx^j$ be a monic multiple of
$\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)$ of degree $D$, let $X\subset\{1,\ldots,D\}$ be
nonempty, and put $c=\max\{|f_{D-d}|:1\le d\le D,\ d\notin X\}$.  Then
$Q=F\big/\prod_{i\le u}(x-r_i)$ is a monic multiple of $P_u$, and every
coefficient of $Q$ at distance $e\ge\max X$ from its leading one satisfies
\[
  |q|\le\frac{c}{\prod_{i\le u}(r_i-1)}\le c .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take $u=1$ first, with $r=r_1$ and $Q=\sum_jq_jx^j$.  Comparing coefficients
in $F=(x-r)Q$ gives $f_j=q_{j-1}-rq_j$, so $q_j=(q_{j-1}-f_j)/r$ and,
unwinding to $q_{-1}=0$,
\[
  q_j=-\sum_{i\le j}f_i\,r^{\,i-j-1}.
\]
The coefficient of $Q$ at distance $e$ from its leading one is $q_{D-1-e}$,
and the $f_i$ that sum reads have $i\le D-1-e$, that is, distance $D-i\ge e+1$
from the leading coefficient of $F$.  If $e\ge\max X$ then none of them is
exempt and none is $f_D$, so each $|f_i|\le c$ and
$|q_{D-1-e}|\le c\sum_{k\ge1}r^{-k}=c/(r-1)$.

For $u>1$ apply that case to $F$ and $r_1$: the quotient $Q_1$ is a monic
multiple of $\prod_{i\ge2}(x-r_i)$ whose coefficients at distance $\ge\max X$
from its leading one are at most $c/(r_1-1)$ in magnitude.  The same
computation with $r_2$ reads only coefficients of $Q_1$ at distance
$\ge\max X+1$, so it applies with $c$ replaced by $c/(r_1-1)$.  Iterating
over $i\le u$ gives the product, and each $r_i-1\ge1$.
\end{proof}

What the lemma leaves uncovered is the head: the $\max X-1$ coefficients of
$Q$ nearer the top than the highest exempt position.  There $q$ is a
geometric combination of the leading coefficients of $F$, the exempt ones
among them, and nothing bounds it termwise.  At a fixed placement that is the
whole question.

\begin{proposition}[The head carries the question]\label{prop:head}
Let $2\le r_1<\cdots<r_L$ be distinct, $0\le u\le L-1$, and $X$ a set of $u$
positive integers.  Suppose that for every $\varepsilon>0$ some monic
multiple $F$ of $\prod_{i=1}^L(x-r_i)$ has $c\le1/\tau(X)+\varepsilon$, with
$c$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:deflate}, and $|q|\le c$ at each coefficient of
$Q=F/\prod_{i\le u}(x-r_i)$ at distance less than $\max X$ from its leading
one.  Then $\tau(X)\le\tau^*$.  If this holds at every $X$ of size $u$ then
$T=\tau^*$ and the infimum of $b_{u+1}$ is $1/\tau^*$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With Lemma~\ref{lem:deflate} the hypothesis reads $b_1(Q)\le c$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:value} applied to the roots $r_{u+1},\ldots,r_L$ with $u=0$
gives $\inf b_1=1/\tau^*$ over monic multiples of $P_u$, the supremum there
running over the single placement $X=\varnothing$.  So
$1/\tau^*\le b_1(Q)\le1/\tau(X)+\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon>0$, that
is $\tau(X)\le\tau^*$.  Taking the supremum over $X$ gives $T\le\tau^*$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:value} gives the reverse.
\end{proof}

The head is a window of $\max X-1$ coefficients, so the hypothesis is a finite
condition at each placement.  Near-extremality in it is not a convenience.  At
$(7,11)$ the multiple $F=(x-7)(x-11)(x+5)=x^3-13x^2-13x+385$ has $b_2(F)=13$
and $X_F=\{3\}$, while $F/(x-7)=x^2-6x-55$ has $b_1=55$: an arbitrary
multiple's head exceeds its $c$ by a factor above four.  How much
near-extremality it needs is exact, and at the cutoff it needs more than there
is.

\begin{proposition}[Reach of the deflation route]\label{prop:defthresh}
Let $u=1$, $X=\{g\}$, and let $F$, $c$ and $Q=F/(x-r_1)$ be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:deflate}.  If the exempt coefficient satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defthresh}
  |f_{D-g}|\le c\,\frac{r_1(r_1-2)}{r_1-1},
\end{equation}
then $b_1(Q)\le c$, and hence $c\ge1/\tau^*$.  Under the weaker hypothesis
$|f_{D-g}|\le c$ the same conclusion is immediate, since $F$ is itself a monic
multiple of $P_1$; \eqref{eq:defthresh} improves on it exactly when
$r_1>\tfrac{3+\sqrt5}2=2.618\ldots$, and at $r_1=2$ it is empty.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $y=1/r_1$ and $w_d=f_{D-d}$, so $|w_d|\le c$ for $1\le d\le D$, $d\ne g$.
Since $F(r_1)=0$, the coefficient of $Q$ at distance $e$ from its leading one
is $-\sum_{k>e}w_ky^{\,k-e}$, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:deflate}.  For $e\ge g$ no
exempt term occurs and the bound $c\,y/(1-y)=c/(r_1-1)\le c$ is the lemma.  For
$e<g$ the exempt term contributes at most $|w_g|y^{\,g-e}$ and the rest at most
$c\,y/(1-y)$, so $b_1(Q)\le c$ as soon as
$|w_g|y^{\,g-e}\le c\bigl(1-\tfrac1{r_1-1}\bigr)=c\,\tfrac{r_1-2}{r_1-1}$ for
every $e<g$.  The largest weight is $y^{\,1}$, at $e=g-1$, which is
\eqref{eq:defthresh}.  Finally $r_1(r_1-2)/(r_1-1)>1$ is $r_1^2-3r_1+1>0$.
\end{proof}

So at every root set with $r_1=2$ --- $(2,3,5,7)$ among them --- no pointwise
bound on the head closes a placement along this route: the head has to be
bounded against $c$ through the cancellation between its terms, not term by
term.

The primal route to the same statement is to insert the zeros one at a time,
each insertion paid for by one node, and it fails outright.  The full
certificate on $(\tfrac13,\tfrac15,\tfrac17)$ with zero set $\{1,3\}$ has
tail $\tfrac{31}{1704}$; adding the node $\tfrac12$ and the zero $2$ gives
the full certificate on all four nodes with the consecutive zero set
$\{1,2,3\}$, of tail $\tfrac1{48}$, so the tail rose although a node was
gained---Theorem~2.2's equality case absorbed it.  Barring consecutive zero
sets does not repair the step.  At $(2,\tfrac94,\tfrac52,3)$ the full
certificate on $(\tfrac49,\tfrac25,\tfrac13)$ with zero set $\{1,4\}$ has
tail $\tfrac{11608}{84555}=0.13728\ldots$, and adding the node $\tfrac12$ and
the zero $2$ gives the zero set $\{1,2,4\}$ and tail
$\tfrac{108}{755}=0.14304\ldots$.  This is what Theorem~\ref{thm:lift} buys
with its hypothesis $X\subseteq Z\cup(g,\infty)$, and why the covering below
it is a search.

\begin{proposition}[Two infima]\label{prop:values}
Over monic $Q\in\R[x]$,
\begin{align*}
  \inf_Qb_2\bigl(Q\,(x-2)(x-3)(x-5)(x-7)\bigr)
    &=\frac{1704}{31}=54.967\ldots,\\
  \inf_Qb_3\Bigl(Q\prod_{r=2}^7(x-r)\Bigr)
    &=\frac{114840}{259}=443.397\ldots,
\end{align*}
against the bounds $48$ and $360$ of \eqref{eq:order} and the values $57$ and
$480$ of the construction.  Neither infimum is attained.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At $(2,3,5,7)$ the full certificate on $(\tfrac13,\tfrac15,\tfrac17)$ with
zero set $\{1,3\}$ is
$v^*_d=(81\cdot3^{-d}-625\cdot5^{-d}+686\cdot7^{-d})/142$.  It has
$v^*_2=-\tfrac1{71}$, is positive past $3$, and has tail $\tfrac{31}{1704}$.
The multiplier $M=x+\tfrac9{62}$ gives
\[
  \frac{\widetilde P_1(y)\,(1+\tfrac9{62}y)}{1-y}
  =1-\tfrac{859}{62}y+\tfrac{1704}{31}y^2-\tfrac{2463}{62}y^3
   -\tfrac{1704}{31}\sum_{d\ge4}y^d ,
\]
so by \eqref{eq:dualpair} $\tau^*=\tfrac{31}{1704}$, with $Z^*=\{1,3\}$ and
$G=\{2\}$.  The full certificate on all four nodes with zero set $\{1,2,5\}$
has tail $\tfrac{21337}{1910352}<\tfrac{31}{1704}$, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:uone} gives the first value.

At $(2,\ldots,7)$ with $u=2$ the full certificate on
$(\tfrac14,\ldots,\tfrac17)$ with zero set $\{1,2,4\}$ has
$v^*_3=\tfrac1{638}$, is negative past $4$, and has tail
$\tfrac{259}{114840}$.  The multiplier $M=x+\tfrac{60}{259}$ gives the
partial sums $1$, $-\tfrac{5379}{259}$, $\tfrac{5666}{37}$,
$-\tfrac{114840}{259}$, $\tfrac{64440}{259}$ and then $\tfrac{114840}{259}$
forever, so $\tau^*=\tfrac{259}{114840}$ with $Z^*=\{1,2,4\}$.  The search
closes in two steps.  Theorem~\ref{thm:lift} with $k=2$ covers every
$X\not\ni3$.  The full certificate on $(\tfrac13,\ldots,\tfrac17)$ with zero
set $\{1,2,3,5\}$, of tail $\tfrac{31}{36720}$, covers every $\{3,x\}$ except
$\{3,4\}$.  The full certificate on all six nodes with zero set
$\{1,2,3,4,7\}$, of tail $\tfrac{119261}{193623120}$, covers $\{3,4\}$.

Neither infimum is attained, because $\tau(X)<\tau^*$ for every $X$.  Each
covering above either lifts with $k\ge1$ onto a padded zero set that is not
consecutive, which is strict by Theorem~\ref{thm:lift}, or has tail below
$\tau^*$ outright.  So every multiple $F$ has
$b_{u+1}(F)\ge1/\tau(X_F)>1/\tau^*$.
\end{proof}

In both cases the extremal series is the construction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:partial} applied to $P_uM$ rather than $P_u$, and the
factor $x+\beta$ has a negative root.  It raises the total $|P_u(1)M(1)|$
above $|P_u(1)|$ but brings every partial sum within it, so \eqref{eq:partial}
holds for the product.  Complementary slackness explains the shape.  Past the
largest zero $g$ of $v^*$ the witness is constant, so $(1-y)W$ is a
polynomial of degree $g+1$ and $\deg M=g+1-m$, which is $1$ both times.

This is the $\ell^\infty$ member of the family this paper works in.  Reduced
length minimizes $\ell^1$ over normalized multiples~\cite{schinzel}.  Its
integer-polynomial $\ell^2$ analogue, the minimal Euclidean norm of an
algebraic number, is effectively computable~\cite{frw}.  The logarithmic
coefficient mass $\Lambda$ of~\cite{coefficient-mass} is additive over
coefficients rather than a norm at all.

\section{Scope}\label{sec:scope}

The bounds here concern coefficient magnitudes, not ordinary length or
height: they are uniform over the degree of $F$ and over all additional real
or complex roots, and in~\cite{coefficient-mass} the hypothesis
$|f_D|\ge1$ enters only where the $\log|f_D|$ term is discarded to reach an
asymptotic.  In the opposite
direction,
the degree of an integer polynomial of height $H\ge2$ all of whose roots are
nonzero rationals is at most a constant times $\log H$~\cite{htv}, and the
minimal height of a degree-$n$ polynomial over a fixed number field with all
roots in $K^\times$ grows exponentially in $n$~\cite{tromp}; the bound here
constrains neither degree nor height but the additive coefficient mass.  The
contribution of~\cite{coefficient-mass} relative to reduced-height duality
is the prefix-sensitive tail
comparison and its iteration; the normalized determinant sequence and its
consecutive-zero evaluation are from Dubickas and Jankauskas~\cite{dj}.
Proposition~\ref{prop:confluent} is supplementary: it carries the tail
theorem to confluent nodes, but Theorem~3.2 already reaches
repeated roots by perturbation, so the main bounds do not depend on it.

In the stated form the coefficient bounds for $k\ge2$ require $r_i\ge2$,
inherited from the node cutoff $y_i\le1/2$ of Theorem~2.2.  The cutoff enters
in exactly two places, the factor $2$ in the correction-ratio bound of
Theorem~2.2's proof and the discard of factors at most $1$ in Lemma~3.1.  Both
matter only for $u\ge1$.  The $k=1$ bound is free of the cutoff, as are
Lemma~2.1 and Lemma~2.3.  The restriction is not an artifact of the proof.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:subtwo}, \eqref{eq:order} is false for $k=2$ already at
$L=2$, and already when only $r_1<2$.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:cutoff}, the
cutoff in the stated product cannot be raised.  Theorem~\ref{thm:repaired}
removes both uses at once by keeping the factors the discard drops, so
\eqref{eq:repaired} holds for every $r_1>1$.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:subtwosharp} it is the infimum when the exempt roots
lie in $(1,2]$ and at most $u$ partial sums of $P$ exceed $|P(1)|$.  With an
exempt root below $2$ and those hypotheses failing, whether it is the infimum
is open.

The pointwise extremal problem asks, for a fixed root sequence and a fixed
$k$, how closely a multiple can approach \eqref{eq:order}.
Corollary~3.6 settles the asymptotic form of the aggregated question for
prime roots, and the multiplier $x+1$ of~\cite[Section~3]{coefficient-mass}
shows that even there the root product need not be the finite-$L$
minimizer.  Whether \eqref{eq:order} is itself the infimum is settled for
distinct roots: by Proposition~\ref{prop:partial} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:converse} it is exactly when \eqref{eq:partial} holds, as
at $(2,3)$ and $(2,3,5)$, where~\cite[Proposition~4.5]{coefficient-mass} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp235} exhibit the multiples, and at $(3,4,5,6)$.
It fails already at $L=3$: at $(2,3,\tfrac72)$ with $u=1$ the partial sums
are $1,-\tfrac{11}2,5$, so the infimum of $b_2$ exceeds $5$.  When
\eqref{eq:partial} fails, Theorem~\ref{thm:value} gives the infimum as $1/T$,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:lift} reduces $T=\tau^*$ to a finite search, which
closes at $(2,3,5,7)$ and $(2,\ldots,7)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:values}) and
at $2119$ further root sets we tried with $u\le4$---integer, rational, and
clustered just above the cutoff---where every one of $40405$ placements inside
$\{1,\ldots,10\}$ is covered by a full certificate whose tail is at most the
least tail of a full certificate on the surviving nodes.  Two things are not
proved in general: that $\tau^*$ is attained by a full certificate with a
witness as in \eqref{eq:dualpair}, and that the search always closes, that is,
that the escaping placement is always extremal.

The second was reduced to one inequality, proved when a single zero of the
minimizer lies above $\max X$.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:step} it is the single
step \eqref{eq:step}, deleting the largest element of the placement; by
Proposition~\ref{prop:stepeasy} the step holds except when the minimizing
zero set straddles $\max X$, and by Proposition~\ref{prop:slide} the
straddling case is the slide inequality \eqref{eq:slide}, which
Proposition~\ref{prop:slidealigned} proves when either minimizer involved sits
at or below $\max X$.  In the remaining case \eqref{eq:slide} fails, at nine
nodes clustered just below the cutoff (Proposition~\ref{prop:slidefails}), so
no argument that ignores the minimality of the zero set being slid can prove
\eqref{eq:step}.  The step itself has no known counterexample: none in
$47638$ instances to $L=6$, in $1352$ further steps, or among all placements
of size $L-2$ inside $\{1,\ldots,15\}$ at the clustered nodes with $L\le8$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:slideinitial} proves the step whenever the minimizing zero
set below $\max X$ is an initial segment, whatever lies above, by smoothing
the minimizing certificate against the new node; what remains is a gap below
$\max X$.  There the displaced zero is not the half-mass point but a shift:
\eqref{eq:shift} moves every zero above $\max X$ up by one, under the
hypothesis that no single such move lowers the tail, which minimality
supplies (Proposition~\ref{prop:shift}).  With one zero above $\max X$ it
is proved, through the chord lemma (Proposition~\ref{prop:chord} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:shiftone}); with several it holds in every one of
$7023$ instances with the hypothesis at $L\le9$ and fails without it, and
it is sharp at the confluent block.  Three alternatives are refuted
above: deleting $\min X$ rather than $\max X$ fails at $(2,3,4,5)$; freeing
$\max Z^-$ rather than the smallest zero above $g$ fails by a factor $1.40$;
and adjoining $g$ to $Z^-$ without sliding, which would need no displaced
zero at all, fails in $245$ of the $379$ straddling instances we put to it,
by as much as a factor $2.01$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:head} states the same
question analytically instead, as a bound on the $\max X-1$ coefficients of a
deflated multiple; by Proposition~\ref{prop:defthresh} that route says nothing
pointwise once $r_1\le\tfrac{3+\sqrt5}2$, which covers every root set with
$r_1=2$.  Repeated roots beyond the confluent pair $(3,3)$ also remain open.
